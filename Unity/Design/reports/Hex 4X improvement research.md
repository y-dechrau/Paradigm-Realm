# Make Paradigm Realm Heard, Fair and Finishable

Paradigm Realm does not need more big systems yet. The best returns are cheap gaps: the game has no sound at all, its difficulty setting does nothing, remembered land is nearly the same brightness as the sea and the clouds, and nothing ends a game that is already decided. After those, the one new system with the strongest case is event cards, because the best-documented cure for a dull mid-game is pressure that keeps changing inside your own empire. Designers across the genre name the late game as the unsolved problem, and Firaxis says more than half of Civ VI players never finished a campaign, so finishing matters more than adding breadth. Paradigm Realm already has much of what the 31 October comparison asked for (ages, unique units, agendas, priests), so that older ranking should be retired. One thing outranks every item: no human has yet played a hex game by hand, so all balance numbers so far come from computer rivals playing each other. No source in this research proves that any single mechanic raises fun or finishing rates; "strong" below means well documented, not proven. None of these recommendations has been built or tried in Unity.

## The ranked list, in plain language

The order weighs how much better the game gets against the size of the job. **Job size** is my estimate of building work, and it leaves out your play-testing and tuning time: Small is under half a day, Medium is 1-3 days, Large is 1-2 weeks or more. **Evidence** is graded as Strong (a designer's own account, an official guideline or a direct measurement of this game), Moderate (several secondary sources, or one good source on one game) or Weak (opinion, forum posts, or my judgement from one screenshot).

### Do not do these

Do not add **era resets or forced changes of civilisation**: Take-Two's chief executive later said Civ VII's changes went too far, and update 1.4.0 made swapping optional ([Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/civilization-vii-civ-firaxis-update.md); [2K patch notes](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2026-may-19-patch-1-4-0/)). Do not convert to **Old World's full Orders system**, which limits how many commands you can give per turn: it would mean rebalancing movement, rivals and every late system. Do not move **rivers onto tile edges**, because river tiles carry your boats and the change touches rules, pathfinding, saves and art. Do not **rewrite the map drawing or interface** without measurements. Do not give rivals **hidden cheats** such as free units in fog or instant wonders. I would also hold aircraft and espionage until the late game has been played, though nothing in the research tests that directly.

## Mechanics: pressure from inside beats more features

**Event cards have the best-documented case.** Soren Johnson, Old World's designer, argues that past early expansion nations become unrealistically stable, and that without pressure other than external conflict the game slides into autopilot ([Notes #7](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-7-characters/); [Notes #8](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-8-opinion/)). Old World answers with **over 3,000 events** run as a virtual deck: each has a trigger, requirements and effects, and the game picks among valid ones by weight. One event can grant a trait that another requires, so writers need no fragile story tree, and Johnson deliberately avoids hidden triggers and effects ([Notes #9](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-9-events/)). Stellaris's anomalies, where each outcome occurs once and can open a chain, are a smaller model ([Stellaris wiki](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Anomaly)). Paradigm Realm does not need 3,000. I would start with a few dozen tied to ages, faith and agendas, record each outcome in the Chronicle, and add a save version for the memory flags. Old World's rate of events is unpublished, so frequency must be tuned in play, and PC Gamer's complaint that flavourful text clashes with abstract numbers is worth remembering when writing them ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/old-world-review/)).

**An early finish attacks the slog directly.** Johnson calls the ending the hardest part of a 4X and says nobody has done it well; his double-score win exists because the player otherwise presses End Turn many more times after victory is certain ([Notes #11](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-11-the-end/)). Civ VII's May 2026 update added a score-lead win: a five-turn countdown rivals can pause, with the required lead falling from 6x through 4x, 3x, 2x and 1.5x to 1.25x late on ([2K patch notes](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2026-may-19-patch-1-4-0/)). That rule is recent and long-term results are unknown. Some players enjoy finishing a won game, so I recommend an offer rather than a forced end. Paradigm Realm has era score but no overall score, so this needs one visible total first. Johnson also warns against a surprise rival victory ending a long game, which the existing stage warnings already address.

**Raiders fill a real hole, but the balance is contested.** The clearest exact rules come from Unciv, an open-source Civ V remake, so they are a working example rather than Firaxis data. Camps appear only on land nobody can see, with a 50% roll per turn, at least 7 tiles from another camp and 4 from a capital; each spawns a unit every 8-12 turns, with none wandering before turn 10 and no ships before turn 30 ([BarbarianManager](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/core/src/com/unciv/logic/automation/civilization/BarbarianManager.kt); [BarbarianEncampment](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/core/src/com/unciv/logic/automation/civilization/BarbarianEncampment.kt)). Civ VI adds a readable warning: a camp's scout finds you, goes home, and then a raid comes ([Civilopedia copy](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/world_6/)). Forum players complain in both directions about how many appear, hence an intensity setting ([CivFanatics](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/barbarian-spawn-rate-remains-insane.628150/)). Computer rivals must also learn to cope with them, and that is the larger part of the job.

**Villages can become a ladder from threat to ally to absorbed.** Endless Legend's 2013 design has minor factions you may crush or pacify through quests and trade, with peace slower but better paying ([EL GDD 5](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/endless-legend/forums/7-archives/threads/3590-el-gdd-5-minor-factions?page=1)). Civ VII lets you spend Influence to befriend Independent Powers, with rivals able to get there first, and then levy or incorporate them ([2K diary](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/dev-diary/diplomacy-influence-trade/)). Unciv's friend-at-30, ally-at-60 scale with decay of 1 per turn is a tested starting point ([DiplomacyManager](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/core/src/com/unciv/logic/civilization/diplomacy/DiplomacyManager.kt)). Building this on the existing villages avoids a separate city-state system, which is my inference rather than something any source tested.

**Difficulty should be open and honest.** Johnson's Civ IV AI had production and research penalties at low levels and bonuses at high ones, closest to even rules at Noble and Prince, and never a wonder bonus ([Slashdot Q&A](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/slashdot-interview-of-the-civ4-team.134142)). He found that small discounts drew little complaint while free units in fog and instantly finished wonders felt unfair: "transparency is the antidote to suspicion and distrust" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/analysis-game-ai-our-cheatin-hearts)). Old World later added an easier bottom level and reworked AI penalties at low difficulty, and its AI fixes targeted clumsy coordination, such as units attacking while healthy neighbours retreated ([Mohawk Update 116](https://mohawkgames.com/2023/11/22/old-world-update-116/)). So the order is: a zero-bonus level for all test games, a level below it, and harder levels that improve rival play before adding production bonuses. Exact percentages elsewhere are thinly sourced, with one machine-translated report giving about 40% at Civ IV's Deity level ([Game Watch](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/20080222/ai.htm)), so yours must come from play. One old project note says no rivals went to war in six test games, and later rival war behaviour needs rechecking.

**The smaller mechanics have thinner support.** Tiered goals (Old World's Ambitions) scale difficulty with progress, yet PC Gamer still found its victory conditions poor and unexplained in 2021 ([Notes #11](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-11-the-end/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/old-world-review/)). Great people are well described but I found little on whether they keep players engaged; Unciv's first costs 100 points and each one after doubles ([GreatPersonManager](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/core/src/com/unciv/logic/civilization/managers/GreatPersonManager.kt)). Undo was among Old World's most popular features ([Johnson](https://www.designer-notes.com/my-elephant-in-the-room-part-1/)). Crises are mixed: Stellaris's soft rising chance (about 24% per five-year check before multipliers, my arithmetic from its [listed weights](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Crisis)) is a good shape, but Millennia's reception was split ([Metacritic](https://legacy.prod.metacritic.com/game/pc/millennia/critic-reviews)) and Ara's act-end elimination raised pressure while its reviewer still reported late monotony ([Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/ara-history-untold-review-pc)).

## Pacing: keep the length and fix the ending

**At about 230 turns, the game's length is not the problem.** Civ VII's official figures are 150-200 turns and 3-4 hours per Age, across three Ages ([Firaxis diary](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/dev-diary/ages)). Old World is reported as capped at 200 turns ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/old-world/civilization-too-long)), and Firaxis's Ed Beach put an average Civ game at 15-20 hours ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/firaxis-big-swing-with-civilization-vii-convincing-players-to-actually-finish-their-games)). At Civ VII's implied pace of roughly 1-1.5 minutes per turn, a 230-turn game runs about 4-6 hours; reaching 15-20 hours would need 4-5 minutes on every turn. That arithmetic is mine and the real turn time is unknown, which is why the diary in #1 comes first. I would not lengthen the game. Turn limits for other games come from community sources and did not hold up well enough to set targets by.

**The last two eras look thin, and the newest figures have drifted.** Your 30 October tuning hit targets of about 36/77/124/170, with earliest Transcendence at 222-250, and the status file records that "Era 5 is not completed before Transcendence". The 5 November hex runs on Small finish later, at turns 35-43, 92-108, 141-166 and 187-189 where the fourth era was reached (one game per setting), which is about 12-41 turns behind the targets from era 2 onwards. **The 222-250 figure predates the hex grid and native peoples, so it needs measuring again.** In the test games run just before the hex change, rivals held only about 92-101 of the 180 technologies at turn 240, a little over half, and hex rivals held 80-84 at turn 200. Computer rivals are not humans, but late content may be going unseen. The choices are to accept a fast finish, to move Transcendence's requirements later, or to trim the late tree. A short, intense finish may be good, and a tree of which half goes unused is cheap to fix once you know how a human plays.

**Measure load rather than guessing it.** Firaxis asked of each system which decisions need asking only every five to ten minutes and which are tiresome clicking ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/firaxis-big-swing-with-civilization-vii-convincing-players-to-actually-finish-their-games)). Warning signs to look for in the diary are rising clicks per turn, a clear leader long before the end, and technologies rarely used. Rivals hold about 4-6 cities in those runs, so if you reach ten or more, computer-only games will flatter the balance. I found no studio publishing its measuring methods, so this is sensible practice rather than a known standard. Shafer's rule on Civ V's happiness also applies when tuning: penalties "should be challenges to overcome, not an insurmountable wall" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/revisiting-the-design-of-civ-5)).

## Textures: fix light and calm before painting more

**Fog is the most measurable visual fault.** On one pair of 5 November screenshots with hand-picked areas, the brightness contrast looks like this:

| Pair | Contrast |
|---|---|
| Visible grass to visible sea | 1.8:1 |
| Visible grass to remembered grass | 3.4:1 |
| Remembered grass to remembered ocean | about 1.25:1 |
| Remembered forest or mountains to remembered ocean | about 1.0-1.1:1 |
| Remembered ocean to unexplored cloud | about 1.04:1 |

Remembered tiles are darkened heavily and then covered by a dark drifting veil (`IsoRenderer.cs`). Xbox's guidelines ask for at least 3:1 on large elements in maps and for a signal other than colour ([XAG 102](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102); [XAG 103](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-gb/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/103)). No source sets numbers for terrain or fog, so these are starting points of my own: reduce colour to about 50-60%, darken less, lighten the veil, and try for remembered land against ocean of 1.5:1 and against cloud of 2:1.

**Ground and water should get fewer colours and more rest.** SLYNYRD's advice is to pair a busy texture with a calmer one, keep few colours per texture, and mix flat areas with patches rather than repeating one design ([Pixelblog 20](https://slynyrd.com/blog/2019/8/27/pixelblog-20-top-down-tiles)). His colour ramps shift hue as they brighten, up to about 20 degrees per step, because brightness-only ramps look flat ([Pixelblog 1](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/1/10/pixelblog-1-color-palettes)). Pixel Parmesan warns that needless dithering (checkerboard speckle) reads as noise and grows more obvious when zoomed ([Dithering](https://pixelparmesan.com/blog/dithering-for-pixel-artists)). In practice that means one hand-chosen set of 4-6 colours per ground type picked by noise, a much quieter open sea with detail kept at shallows and foam, and no dither as ground texture. This is craft opinion, written mostly for tiles of 32 x 32 or smaller rather than your 64 x 40, so judge it by side-by-side screenshots. Because ground is generated in code, none of it needs SpriteCook credits, which last stood at 8 in the project notes.

**Readability comes from shape.** Civ VI's art director says players zoom out, so silhouettes must read from a distance ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/making-i-civilization-vi-i-both-playable-and-beautiful)). Wesnoth's fixed layer order, where a higher terrain always draws its edge over a lower one, is a useful check on your blended borders ([Wesnoth](https://wiki.wesnoth.org/Tiles_Tutorial)). Outlined markers hold on light and dark ground ([XAG 102](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102)), so they may replace the grey squares behind resources, though only if a comparison shows no loss of clarity. Red Blob Games warns that noise-built maps tend to feel the same everywhere, and supports drawing rivers wider downstream ([Terrain from Noise](https://www.redblobgames.com/maps/terrain-from-noise/); [River growing](https://www.redblobgames.com/x/1723-procedural-river-growing/)); look at whole worlds to judge this.

**Map-drawing speed must be measured before anything is rebuilt.** Unity says its older interface system (IMGUI, which draws your map) is not intended for normal player interfaces and may run several times per frame ([Unity manual](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/manual/uitoolkits/ui-imgui/guiscripting-guide); [OnGUI](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/script-reference/unityengine/monobehaviour/ongui)). I found no official cost figure for this kind of drawing. So the sensible step is a profile on a Massive map at furthest zoom in a real build, and a cheaper fog overlay meanwhile. A rewrite is justified only by that result.

## Everything else: sound, saves, access and first games

**Sound is the cheapest large change.** A 32-person study of one non-strategy game found character and ambient sounds affected flow and fun, though I saw only its abstract and found no 4X study ([Applied Sciences](https://mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/2/583)). Start with interface effects and build separate volume sliders from day one, since guidelines rank these as basic ([Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)). For music, use only CC0 or CC BY tracks, log each track's source and licence, and add a Credits screen; Incompetech's default licence requires visible credit ([Incompetech FAQ](https://incompetech.com/music/royalty-free/faq.html)). Non-commercial licences would block a future sale, and because your repository is public, check that each licence allows the files to be published there. Vet any chants too: Sony recalled LittleBigPlanet over Qur'anic lines in a licensed song ([MTV](https://www.mtv.com/news/wntrxa/littlebigplanet-release-delayed-over-quotes-from-quran)).

**Saves have an easy first win.** A turn-30 autosave of 7.7 million characters compressed to 91,463 bytes in 57 ms, about **84:1**, outside Unity; 83% of the file is tiles, and most of a tile's text is repeated field names. Late saves will compress less, and this has not been run in Unity. Old saves can still load, because the first byte shows which kind of file it is. Packing tile data more tightly is a second, larger step, worth doing only if Unity measurements show saving is slow.

**Accessibility fits a turn-based game well.** Turn-based play already avoids timing barriers. Colour independence, readable fonts, remapping and a wide choice of difficulty remain, and all are rated Basic by the guidelines ([full list](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)). Red or green difficulty affects about 8-10% of males, and free colour choice is the most requested option ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-colour-alone/)). A game with more than eight civilisations outruns the eight colours of a standard colour-safe palette, so flags and two-tone borders must carry identity. Allowing difficulty changes during play is a useful follow-up. Controller and screen-reader support are later and larger jobs.

**First-game advice is reasoned rather than evidenced.** The only solid figure is Beach's "less than 50 percent" for finishing Civ VI, supported by Steam's 37.4% first-win achievement ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/firaxis-big-swing-with-civilization-vii-convincing-players-to-actually-finish-their-games); [Steam stats](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/289070/achievements)). I found no reliable data on tutorial drop-off in PC 4X games. Mohawk spread out tutorial prompts and added an easier level ([Update 116](https://mohawkgames.com/2023/11/22/old-world-update-116/)), and guidelines rate objective reminders and contextual help as intermediate. A small map, few rivals and an easy level by default seem the natural first game. Interface polish is a warning from Civ VII: Firaxis called the UI its top priority after launch ([Firaxis](https://civilization.2k.com/en-GB/civ-vii/news/message-from-firaxis-games/)).

## How far to trust all this

**The evidence is good on what designers tried and poor on what worked.** Strongest are designers' own accounts (Johnson's notes, Firaxis's diaries and patch notes, Shafer's Civ V retrospective), official guidelines and direct measurements of this project. Moderate are reviews and open-source code from other games. Weakest are forum claims, excerpts and my own screenshot judgements. No telemetry links any mechanic to retention, and Firaxis's completion figure does not show why players stopped. A third-party benchmark puts the non-strategy median at about 36% from a small sample, which suggests Civ VI's rate may not be unusual ([Two Average Gamers](https://www.twoaveragegamers.com/game-completion-rates/)). Old World dominates the mechanics evidence, and its creator wrote much of it, so it is a persuasive account from one team rather than independent proof. PC Gamer's 2021 review also predates later patches.

**Numbers in the 31 October comparison should not be reused as they stand.** Its "1000+" Old World events is out of date. Its great-person costs, raider numbers, era thresholds and event odds have no source and are only proposals. Its Civ VII great-works entry should not be relied on. Its ranking is also overtaken, since era score, unique units and buildings, agendas and priests are now built.

**Gaps remain.** The research found nothing usable on Endless Space 2's Senate, no Firaxis account of what failed in Civ VI, no exact Civ VI barbarian rates or wonder refunds, and no official Civ V-VII difficulty tables. Steam achievements, modding and Workshop support were not researched. Several sources were read through a summarising tool or as excerpts, so check quotations before reprinting them. All job sizes are my estimates.

## Civilisation source notes, for the separate writing

These notes feed other writing and are only summarised here. They gather 16-19 source motifs for each of five worlds, tagged either free to echo (extinct traditions and literature) or needing care (living religions and peoples). **Fae Wilds** draws on Irish, Welsh, Arthurian and Norse otherworlds: peoples who withdrew, time slips, and debts repaid exactly. **Ashen Waste** draws on biblical desolation imagery, 1 Enoch's giants and Norse world-burning. **Infernal Depths** draws on Solomonic demon lore, Dante and Milton for an organised, legalistic underworld. **Plague Marshes** draws on Mesopotamian disease demons, marsh lights and fen monsters. **Drowned Deep** draws on sea monsters, Ys and the fish-cloaked Apkallu sages, which feel fresher than Atlantis.

The working rules are to use invented names throughout, keep sacred figures and living faiths' holy places out, quote no scripture, write "the old tales" rather than claiming scriptural authority, and search every invented name against protected names such as Skaven or Fremen. Past complaints centred on playable sacred figures, holy sites as battlegrounds and misused scripture ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/hindu-leader-requests-removal-of-dieties-from-smite-hi-rez-declines/); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/eidos-responds-to-sikh-controversy/1100-2897279)). The notes lean on Wikipedia, so check verse numbers before use, and no sensitivity readers were consulted. They also flag that the 23 real faiths already in the game raise the same questions and may merit their own review.

Two points connect this to game design. The gates are locked and **there is no way to open them in play**, so players would never meet those peoples unless that changes. The parallel worlds already make a map about six times larger, and 14 civilisations took 217 ms a turn on Large in test games. Adding dozens more peoples would raise turn time and save size by an amount nobody has measured.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the instinct that a 4X improves by gaining systems. Paradigm Realm is already broad for a project at this stage; what it lacks is what a player meets in the first hour and the last: sound, a fair opponent, a readable map and an ending that knows when to stop. Those fixes are also the ones least likely to be wasted, because they stay useful whatever play-testing reveals.

The next decision is yours: whether a game should build towards a short, sharp finish or show everything in the technology tree. The evidence cannot settle that, but one played game can begin to. The sealed worlds are the wild card. I found no comparable feature in the games reviewed, so there is no evidence on how they will play, which makes opening the gates the largest untested design question in the project.
