# Which mechanics would most improve Paradigm Realm: evidence from recent and classic 4X games

Evidence labels used below. [PRIMARY] is a designer's own writing, official notes or diary, the game's own text, or source code. [MIRROR] is civilopedia.net, which appears to be a third-party copy of the in-game Civilopedia. [SECONDARY] is a review or guide. [EXCERPT] means I saw only a search excerpt, not the page. [COMMUNITY] is forum opinion. The Civilization Fandom wiki returned HTTP 402 and 403, so nothing rests on it directly. All mechanics are recorded in general terms as inspiration; none should be copied as content. [LINK?] means the search tool returned the claim without tying it clearly to one result, so the link is my best match and should be opened before the claim is reused.

## What do Old World, Humankind, Civ VI and VII, Endless Legend/Space, Age of Wonders 4, Millennia, Ara, Unciv, Freeciv and Stellaris do that designers or reviews say worked or failed?

### Takeaway
The best-documented successes create pressure from inside the empire, or force a trade-off every turn (Old World events, characters and opinion; Orders). The best-documented failures are systems that break continuity (Civ VII's forced civ switching and age resets) or are thin at the end of the game (Millennia's Crisis and Variant Ages, Humankind's and Ara's late game). A strong theme: developers themselves name the late game as the unsolved problem.

### Cited Findings

**Old World: Orders**
- Orders are a limited resource spent to command units and characters; the designer's aim was to replace the rule that every unit moves every turn, because that rule gives no reason to skip an action and so creates late-game busywork [PRIMARY] — [Johnson, My Elephant in the Room, Part 1](https://www.designer-notes.com/my-elephant-in-the-room-part-1/)
- Most units move three times a turn; moving further needs a costly Forced March. Freely buying and selling Orders warped early multiplayer tests and was removed; buying and stockpiling Orders moved to late-game laws [PRIMARY] — [same](https://www.designer-notes.com/my-elephant-in-the-room-part-1/)
- Johnson treats a need for automation as a warning sign that the automated activity is badly designed [PRIMARY] — [same](https://www.designer-notes.com/my-elephant-in-the-room-part-1/)
- Undo was among the most popular features in reviews and also helped debugging. Tooltips that open from other tooltips are also credited as a success [PRIMARY] — [same](https://www.designer-notes.com/my-elephant-in-the-room-part-1/). PC Gamer independently praises undo of a move or whole turn [SECONDARY] — [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/old-world-review/)
- Orders per turn rise with Legitimacy, which ties character events to tempo [SECONDARY] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/old-world-review/)
- A fan analysis says late-game Order surpluses reduce the system's bite, and a forum thread conversely asks if there are too few. Weak evidence either way [EXCERPT, COMMUNITY] — [Old World Deep Dives](https://cf-inc.shapes.inc/fandom/old-world/deep-dive); [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/597180/discussions/0/3421063428080814745)
- Even with Orders, PC Gamer's reviewer was still tired of moving units one by one in war, so Orders did not remove all war tedium [SECONDARY] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/old-world-review/)

**Old World: characters, families, opinion**
- Johnson's "Eternal China Syndrome": past early expansion, nations become unrealistically and unfunly stable, internal pressures are only taxes in another form, and decisions cluster early so the late path becomes predictable [PRIMARY] — [Designer Notes #7](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-7-characters/)
- Characters were added because people like games about people, not as an ECS fix, but he says they do counter it. Leader powers disappear on death; there are ten archetypes, each power active under 10% of the time; heirs are only partly controllable [PRIMARY] — [same](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-7-characters/)
- A new leader resets relationships, and players accept this where they resent seemingly random AI swings [PRIMARY] — [same](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-7-characters/)
- Without pressure other than external conflict the game slides into auto-pilot, so families and religions each hold an opinion of the player. Friendly families cut city Maintenance, angry ones weaken their units, and very unhappy ones may raise rebels that can capture cities. Modifiers differ per family, so pleasing one annoys another [PRIMARY] — [Designer Notes #8](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-8-opinion/)
- Known weakness: family identity remained hard to communicate on units [PRIMARY] — [same](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-8-opinion/). PC Gamer found the UI overwhelming and religion spread hard to find [SECONDARY] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/old-world-review/)

**Old World: ending**
- Johnson calls the ending the hardest part of a 4X and says nobody has done it well; once victory is certain the player still has to press End Turn many more times [PRIMARY] — [Designer Notes #11](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-11-the-end/)
- Rejected: themed culture, religion and diplomacy victories, as they force predetermined play. Used: ten Ambitions drawn from tiered decks so difficulty follows progress; Ambitions belong to leaders and heirs get a limited time to finish them; victory points act as a race timer; doubling the next-closest score wins early [PRIMARY] — [same](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-11-the-end/)
- Yet PC Gamer said the game peters out and the victory conditions are poor and mostly unexplained (its 2021 version: 52 points within 200 years) [SECONDARY] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/old-world-review/). A 2021-era review; later patches not checked.

**Civ VII**
- Firaxis's own guide says late-game Civ is not much fun and that snowballing makes choices barely matter, and gives this as the case for Ages: "more than half of the Civilization VI players have never finished a single campaign" [PRIMARY] — [2K: Ages explanation](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/gameplay/ages-explanation)
- Design: three Ages, each planned at about 150 to 200 turns on standard speed and 3 to 4 hours; each ends in a staged Crisis with negative Crisis Policies chosen by the player. Legacy Path milestones speed up age progress and give points to spend at transition; a complete path gives a Golden Age Legacy and missing milestones a Dark Age Legacy. Tech and civic trees restart; old buildings stay at reduced yields [PRIMARY; the page says it predates later updates] — [same](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/gameplay/ages-explanation)
- Take-Two's CEO later said the company got it wrong and the formula change went too far for consumers [SECONDARY, quoting Zelnick to Game File] — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/civilization-vii-civ-firaxis-update.md)
- Update 1.4.0 (19 May 2026): players may stay or swap at each transition. Victories now span all Ages, because the old system squeezed the Modern Age into a short, linear race [PRIMARY] — [2K patch notes 1.4.0](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2026-may-19-patch-1-4-0/)
- A new score-lead victory: reaching a lead over second place starts a five-turn countdown that rivals can pause by closing the gap. The required lead falls as time passes: 6x, 4x, 3x, 2x, 1.5x, then 1.25x in the last stretch [PRIMARY] — [same](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2026-may-19-patch-1-4-0/)
- Player complaints: the reset is harsh and feels like loosely connected games; the Crisis does little against snowballing [COMMUNITY, EXCERPT, LINK?] — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1295660/discussions/0/599644333221660784). About 4,000 joined the test branch and one outlet reports the best Steam peak in over a year [SECONDARY, EXCERPT, LINK?, not checked] — [The FPS Review](https://www.thefpsreview.com/?p=150766)

**Humankind**
- Each era has 21 stars across seven categories, seven are needed to advance, Fame ranks everyone, and keeping a culture forgoes new traits for more Fame [SECONDARY] — [GameWatcher review, 7.5/10](https://www.gamewatcher.com/humankind/review)
- The same review says stacked culture changes feel like piles of passive bonuses without coherent identity, diplomacy is unresponsive, and religion is easy to forget [SECONDARY] — [same](https://www.gamewatcher.com/humankind/review)
- A later retrospective judges the late game less polished than the early game, suggests it missed the open-beta testing the early game had, and says the pollution system was punitive enough that updates disabled it by default [SECONDARY] — [Matchstick Eyes, 2024](https://www.matchstickeyes.com/2024/08/25/revisiting-humankind-three-years-on/)
- Exclusive culture picks meant some cultures rarely got played [COMMUNITY, EXCERPT, LINK?] — [CivFanatics thread](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ7-made-me-like-humankind.692215)

**Millennia**
- A choice of upcoming Ages is the signature idea, but one nation's choice forces it on all, so a clean nation may still suffer plague. Rushing to the next Age competes with finishing current techs [SECONDARY; review in progress, about two-thirds of one game] — [Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/millennia/review)
- Critics are divided on Crisis Ages; a Metacritic summary calls Variant and Crisis Ages underdeveloped, and PC Gamer says the late game has the same problems as Civ [EXCERPT; the PC Gamer page would not load] — [Metacritic](https://legacy.prod.metacritic.com/game/pc/millennia/critic-reviews); [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/strategy/millennia-review)
- Late-game performance is a repeated complaint; Softpedia advises medium maps with six or seven civs [EXCERPT] — [Softpedia](https://news.softpedia.com/news/millennia-review-537520.shtml)
- Paradox ended development [SECONDARY, EXCERPT] — [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/historical-4x-game-millennia-gets-final-update-paradox-shelves-further-development); [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/millennia/strategy-giant-paradox-calls-time-on-its-struggling-civilization-rival-millennia)

**Ara: History Untold**
- Three acts; at each act's end the lowest-Prestige nations are removed. The Digital Trends reviewer credits this with keeping pressure on, but still lists a repetitive late game: passive resource income, AI repeating the same deals, about 700 turns of automated monotony [SECONDARY] — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/ara-history-untold-review-pc)
- Another review disagrees and calls it polished and compelling [SECONDARY, EXCERPT] — [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/ara-history-untold-review)

**Age of Wonders 4**
- Research is 54 themed tomes across 6 affinities; tome picks change spells, cities and units; factions have no fixed research path [PRIMARY] — [Dev Diary 1](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/age-of-wonders-4/news/age-of-wonders-4-dev-diary-1). New tome tiers open after 2 more tomes of any tier [PRIMARY, EXCERPT] — [SteamDB patch note](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/11674441)
- Free-city ladder is in the next question.

**Stellaris**
- Developer reasoning for all-military crises: any empire reaching the endgame should be able to field a military response [COMMUNITY paraphrase of Dev Diary 30; the diary itself would not load] — [DD 30 thread](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/stellaris-dev-diary-30-late-game-crises.921629/page-24)
- A 2021 diary proposed using Unity as the management resource with unavoidable sprawl penalties, offset by spending Unity [PRIMARY, EXCERPT; a proposal, current state not checked] — [DD 215](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/stellaris-dev-diary-215-gameplay-themes-balancing-considerations.1478888/latest)
- Late-game slowdown is a long-running complaint [COMMUNITY, anecdotal] — [Performance megathread](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/performance-megathread.1253705/page-22)

**Endless Legend, Unciv, Freeciv:** see the next question.

### Inferences
- The recurring failure is not "too many systems" but systems that interrupt continuity or lack late-game testing. Paradigm Realm's own late game should be playtested before more late content is added; CLAUDE.md says interactive play remains unverified.
- Full Orders would be a poor fit: it needs a rebalance of movement, AI and every late system. Its cheaper lessons are Undo, tooltips, and reasons not to act.
- Paradigm's five sealed worlds are an unusual asset. Nothing found here shows an equivalent in the games reviewed, so there is no evidence for how it plays.

### Gaps
- Endless Space 2: I found no usable source on the Senate or its reception.
- Civ VI's own designers on what failed: no primary postmortem found. GDC Vault was not reached; no talk is cited here.
- Jon Shafer: found only biography and interview coverage, none stating Civ V or At the Gates lessons usably ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/jon-shafer-interview-at-the-gates/) not read). His reversal to stacking is one French excerpt only.
- Reviews are subjective and several come from excerpts; no retention or sales data links any single mechanic to success.

## How do barbarians or independent powers, city-states, event systems, great people and wonders work in practice, with exact numbers where official sources give them?

### Takeaway
Official Civ VI and VII text describes these systems mostly without numbers. The only exact, inspectable numbers here are Unciv's open source (a Civ V remake, not Firaxis data), Stellaris's wiki, and official patch notes. The design pattern that recurs and fits Paradigm is a ladder: threat, then relationship, then absorption.

### Cited Findings

**Barbarians and independents**
- Civ VI: outposts may appear on any tile no unit can see; Naval, Cavalry (near Horses) and Melee types. Each first makes a Scout; when it spots you it returns and the outpost builds a raid. Destroying one gives Gold. No figures given [MIRROR] — [Civilopedia: Barbarians](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/world_6/)
- Civ VI optional clans: seven types. Hire (permanent unit, Gold), Bribe (they leave your land and do not target your cities for some turns; cost scales with your city count), Incite against a rival (dear but does not expire), Ransom captured civilians, Raid (Gold, outpost stays), Disperse (destroys, gives XP). Progress toward becoming a city-state grows with Food on nearby unowned tiles; hiring and bribing speed it, raiding and inciting slow it; conversions are capped. No numeric values [MIRROR] — [Civilopedia: Clans](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/clans/). One writer expected more barbarians from the mode [SECONDARY, EXCERPT] — [Gaming Nexus](https://www.gamingnexus.com/News/46625/I-expected-Civilization-VIs-Barbarian-Clans-game-mode-to-have-morebarbarians)
- Spawn rate is a persistent complaint in both directions; forum accounts of raid size conflict [COMMUNITY] — [CivFanatics](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/barbarian-spawn-rate-remains-insane.628150/); [Rules thread](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/rules-for-barbarian-spawns.654941/latest)
- Official notes list a Gold value of 300, rising to 500 after Metal Casting and 700 after Steel, up from 100. It appears under the Julius Caesar entry, so it reads as a leader ability, not the base bounty [PRIMARY; my reading] — [2K Civ VI update notes](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vi/update-notes)
- Unciv's exact rules [PRIMARY code, not Firaxis data]:
  - Camps per turn after the first: a 50% roll, then at most one. Only on land nobody can see. Target = unseen land tiles divided by (all tiles to the power 0.4), minus existing camps; turn 1 places a third of that. Not within 7 tiles of another camp, 4 of a destroyed one, or 4 of a major capital. No wandering units before turn 10, none if more than 2 barbarians within 4 tiles, naval after turn 30 — [BarbarianManager.kt](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/core/src/com/unciv/logic/automation/civilization/BarbarianManager.kt)
  - Each camp spawns every 8 to 12 turns, halved in the raging option, plus a difficulty delay, minus up to 3 as it spawns more. Attacking a camp halves its countdown; a destroyed one blocks nearby spawns for 15 turns — [BarbarianEncampment.kt](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/core/src/com/unciv/logic/automation/civilization/BarbarianEncampment.kt)
- Freeciv: instead of permanent camps, uprisings happen at a random spot between minimum and maximum distances from the nearest city; chance depends on the victim's size, government civil-war chance and difficulty [PRIMARY code docs; constant values not on the page] — [Freeciv barbarian.c docs](https://files.freeciv.org/nightly/weekly/S3_3/doxygen/html/dd/d14/barbarian_8c.html)
- Endless Legend (2013 pre-release design post): one to three villages per minor faction in a region; neutral villages field guards and roaming raiders that damage resource buildings. Force requires destroying all villages; peace needs quests or trade, is slower and leaves room for rivals but pays better. Pacified villages stop raiding and add Population; losing the region loses pacification. Assimilation gives a trait and a recruitable unit, limited by slots; each faction only once. The stated aim is that they stay a challenge into mid-game [PRIMARY] — [EL GDD 5](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/endless-legend/forums/7-archives/threads/3590-el-gdd-5-minor-factions?page=1). Later values not verified; [wiki.gg](https://endlesslegend.wiki.gg/wiki/Strategy_Guide/Minor_Factions) not read
- Humankind: Patronage rises by paying money or Influence; at about 90 to 100% you can assimilate, and absorbed cities still count against the city cap. They cannot win Fame. A very unstable player city can break away and become independent [SECONDARY guide, EXCERPT] — [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/humankind/independent-people/z0fa45)

**City-states**
- Civ VI: 1 envoy gives a capital bonus (+2 of the type, or +4 Gold); 3 add a bonus per matching district; 6 add it again. Suzerain needs at least 3 envoys and the most [SECONDARY, 2020 page] — [Well of Souls](https://well-of-souls.com/civ/civ6_overview.html). Tied civs have no suzerain; suzerains get its resources and may pay to levy its units [SECONDARY, EXCERPT] — [Civ wiki](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/City-state_(Civ6)). In-game text was not read: [Envoys](https://civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/citystates_4/), [Suzerain](https://civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/citystates_5/)
- Civ VII: Influence is spent to befriend an Independent Power; rivals can get there first and lock you out. Afterwards a suzerain can grow borders or military, levy units (kept permanently), incorporate it as a settlement, or incite raids. A few have a resource only the suzerain can get. No numbers in the diary. The stated reason is to make diplomacy about large decisions [PRIMARY] — [2K diary](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/dev-diary/diplomacy-influence-trade/). One player thinks suzerain bonuses too weak [COMMUNITY] — [CivFanatics](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/what-additional-suzerain-bonuses-from-city-states-would-you-like-to-see-in-civilization-vii-in-the-future.694297)
- Unciv (comments cite Civ V's source) [PRIMARY code]: Friend at 30 Influence, Ally at 60 for the top civ, enemy below 0, floor -60. Decay 1 per turn above the resting point, 1.5 for hostile personalities, 2 for aggressors, 25% slower when sharing a religion — [DiplomacyManager.kt](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/core/src/com/unciv/logic/civilization/diplomacy/DiplomacyManager.kt). Gold gift gives gold^1.01 / 9.8, shrinking by up to two-thirds as the game progresses to turn 400, rounded down to a multiple of 5, minimum 5. Breaking protection costs 20 Influence and a 20-turn block. Friendly great-person gift every 37 to 43 turns — [CityStateFunctions.kt](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/core/src/com/unciv/logic/civilization/diplomacy/CityStateFunctions.kt)
- Age of Wonders 4: gift a Whispering Stone to raise Allegiance, then Pacts of Cooperation, Loyalty and Vassalage; one overlord only. Vassal shares 30% of Gold, Mana, Knowledge and gives 2 Rally points; top tier 50% and 5 points; integration from the third tier for Imperium scaled by population. Integrated cities count against the city cap, vassals do not [SECONDARY; no thresholds] — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/age-of-wonders-4-how-to-gain-and-integrate-vassals/). Players argue vassals often beat integration [COMMUNITY, LINK?] — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1669000/discussions/0/4628105955353058049)

**Event systems**
- Old World has over 3,000 events (the earlier comparison's "1000+" is out of date). Events are a virtual deck: each has a trigger, requirements and effects. On a trigger the game collects all valid events and picks by weight, probability and priority. Subjects are game objects (characters, cities, families, laws, techs); options can depend on leader traits; chains allow multi-step events [PRIMARY] — [Designer Notes #9](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-9-events/)
- The system is loosely coupled: one event grants a trait, another requires it, so writers work in parallel with no fragile tree. Johnson avoided opaque triggers and effects, since transparency is central to his design. Civ IV's scripted random events were an evolutionary dead end not kept in Civ V. No per-turn frequency is published [PRIMARY] — [same](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-9-events/)
- Multiplayer players, expected to reject randomness, kept events on [EXCERPT of Johnson] — [CivFanatics link to notes](https://civfanatics.com/?p=5932). Events can affect Legitimacy and hence Orders [SECONDARY] — [Stove review](https://page.onstove.com/quarter/gb/view/12242666)
- PC Gamer says flavourful event text clashes with abstract numbers in the UI [SECONDARY] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/old-world-review/). Late event timing draws one user complaint [COMMUNITY, EXCERPT, LINK?] — [GOG](https://www.gog.com/en/game/old_world?page=9)
- Stellaris anomalies: science ships find them on surveys; each outcome occurs once; outcomes include choices, research, deposits and event chains (wiki verified for version 4.5; discovery chance not in the part read) [wiki] — [Stellaris wiki: Anomaly](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Anomaly)
- Stellaris end-game crises: a check runs every 5 years after the chosen endgame year. Weights are 120 for nothing, 8 for one crisis, 10 for each of three others, multiplied up to ×4 by elapsed time. Crises in the all-crisis setting are 25+ years apart; each defeated one doubles later strength. Mid-game crises exist, tied to DLC [wiki] — [Stellaris wiki: Crisis](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Crisis)

**Great people**
- Civ VI: each type has its own points; the cost depends on that person's era; districts, buildings and Production projects give points; Gold or Faith buys early, priced by missing points; only major civs earn them; surplus converts to Faith when none remain [MIRROR] — [Civilopedia: Great People](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/rise-and-fall/concepts/greatpeople_2/). Point sources such as +1 to +5 from wonders are [SECONDARY, EXCERPT] — [Civ wiki](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Great_Scientist_(Civ6))
- Unciv's Civ V-style numbers: first of a pool costs 100 points times game speed and the cost doubles each time; generals start at 200, +50 each [PRIMARY code] — [GreatPersonManager.kt](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/core/src/com/unciv/logic/civilization/managers/GreatPersonManager.kt)
- Old World makes characters serve this role, and powers end with death; see above.
- Civ VI boosts: Eureka gives 50% of a tech's science [SECONDARY] — [Well of Souls](https://well-of-souls.com/civ/civ6_overview.html)

**Wonders**
- Civ VI: fills a hex with placement requirements, within 3 tiles of a city centre; only the first finisher benefits; losers get part of their Production and some Gold back; completion expands borders by 2 tiles. No refund amounts [MIRROR] — [Civilopedia: Wonders](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/cities_6/)

### Inferences
- Worked example from Unciv's formula (my arithmetic): a 2,000-tile map has a divisor of about 20, so 1,200 unseen land tiles allow up to 60 camps before spacing, which is a generous upper limit. Spacing of 7 tiles is doing the real limiting.
- Worked example from Stellaris's base weights (my arithmetic): 38 out of 158, about 24% per 5-year check before multipliers if all four are eligible. A rising-chance check gives a soft start without a fixed date.
- Paradigm's tribal villages already resemble the first rung of a threat-relationship-absorption ladder. City-states could be the top rung of that same ladder rather than a separate system.
- The earlier comparison's great-person "+60 each" and era/raider numbers have no source I found. Treat them as proposals only.

### Gaps
- Civ VI exact spawn rates, envoy sources, great-person point totals and wonder refund percentages: no official numbers reached. Base bounty also unverified.
- Civ VII Independent Power numbers, and whether Great Works were replaced as the earlier comparison's table says. Test of Time coverage refers to AI tuning for Great People, so do not reuse that cell.
- Humankind's patronage threshold rests on one guide. Freeciv's constant values need a look at its source or rulesets. Old World's event frequency is unpublished.

## Which mechanics fix mid-game boredom and late-game busywork?

### Takeaway
No source claims a full solution, and Johnson says no one has done endings well. The most evidenced tools are: internal pressure that keeps changing, goals that scale with progress, ending the game once decided, and removing per-unit chores at design level. Crises and resets have mixed or negative records.

### Cited Findings
- Internal pressure: without it the game drifts to auto-pilot; opinions and impermanent leader powers keep the state changing [PRIMARY] — [Notes #8](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-8-opinion/); [Notes #7](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-7-characters/)
- Events disrupt the steady flow of play, such as a sudden need to defend a city [PRIMARY] — [Notes #9](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-9-events/)
- Goals that scale: tiered Ambitions; a reviewer credits them for keeping the late game interesting [PRIMARY] — [Notes #11](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-11-the-end/); [SECONDARY, EXCERPT, LINK?] — [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/old-world-review)
- Ending earlier: Old World's double-score win targets End Turn pressing after victory is certain [PRIMARY] — [Notes #11](https://www.designer-notes.com/old-world-designer-notes-11-the-end/). Civ VII's score-lead countdown with a falling threshold does the same across the game [PRIMARY] — [2K 1.4.0](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2026-may-19-patch-1-4-0/). A blogger's suggestion: announce when the outcome is settled and offer to stop or simulate [opinion] — [Game Developer, Bart Stewart 2017](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/five-suggestions-for-4x-fun)
