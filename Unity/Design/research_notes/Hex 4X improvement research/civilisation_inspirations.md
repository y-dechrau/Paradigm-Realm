# Source Motifs for 50 Invented Civilisations in Five Sealed Worlds

Tags used on every motif. **[FREE]** = extinct tradition, legend or literature; names and figures can be echoed fairly freely. **[CARE]** = tied to a living religion or living people; borrow the abstract image only, invent all names. **Text** = what the source itself says. **Later** = what commentary or later tradition added. These tags and every "hook" are my judgement, not statements by the sources.

## Fae Wilds (enchanted forests, silver lakes): which source motifs fit?

### Takeaway
Irish, Welsh, Arthurian and Norse otherworld material gives the richest stock, nearly all of it medieval literature rather than living worship. The recurring ideas are peoples who withdrew rather than died, time running differently, and rulers bound by strange laws.

### Cited Findings
1. **Arrival of the Tuatha Dé Danann [FREE].** Text (*Lebor Gabála Érenn*): they arrive in dark clouds, burn their ships and bring three days of darkness. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuatha_D%C3%A9_Danann)
2. **The four northern cities [FREE].** Falias, Gorias, Murias and Finias each supply one treasure: the Dagda's cauldron, Lugh's spear, the Lia Fáil and the Claíomh Solais. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuatha_D%C3%A9_Danann)
3. **Retreat into the mounds [FREE].** After defeat by the Milesians, Manannán leads them underground into the sídhe mounds, a retreat the article links to Tír na nÓg. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuatha_D%C3%A9_Danann)
4. **Demoted gods [FREE].** Later: medieval Christian writers recast them as fallen angels or as humans skilled in magic. They are often read as Ireland's pre-Christian gods. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuatha_D%C3%A9_Danann)
5. **Tír na nÓg and the time slip [FREE].** Oisín spends what seems three years there; about 300 pass in Ireland. Warned not to touch the ground, he falls from the horse, ages at once and dies. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%ADr_na_n%C3%93g)
6. **Ways into the Otherworld [FREE].** The *echtrae* and *immram* tales reach it through burial mounds, mist, underwater travel or a sea crossing of three days. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%ADr_na_n%C3%93g)
7. **Annwn [FREE].** The Welsh Otherworld, ruled by Arawn or Gwyn ap Nudd, a place of delight and youth without disease. In the *Mabinogi*, Pwyll swaps places with Arawn for a year. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annwn)
8. **Raid on the cauldron [FREE].** In *Preiddeu Annwfn*, Arthur sails to Annwn for a prisoner or a cauldron and only seven return. Sites include Caer Pedryvan, a four-cornered fort. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annwn)
9. **Avalon [FREE].** Geoffrey of Monmouth (*Historia*, c. 1136) first names it: Arthur's sword is forged there and he is taken there to recover. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalon)
10. **Nine sisters' law [FREE].** *Vita Merlini* (c. 1150) calls it the Isle of Fruit Trees, ruled by nine sisters under Morgen. Later: the Glastonbury "discovery" of Arthur's bones (c. 1190) is generally considered a likely fraud. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalon)
11. **Lake that is an illusion [FREE].** In the Vulgate cycle the Lady of the Lake lives in an enchanted realm hidden behind an illusory lake and fosters Lancelot. Names vary (Nimue, Viviane, Niniane); she imprisons Merlin in tree, cave or stone. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_of_the_Lake)
12. **Seelie and Unseelie [FREE].** Scottish folklore: the seely folk repay kindness but avenge insults; the darker group attack unprovoked. The Old Scots root *sele* means good fortune. The two-court scheme is largely later. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seelie)
13. **Álfheimr, light and dark elves [FREE].** *Grímnismál* 5: given to Freyr as a tooth-gift. *Gylfaginning* 17: light elves there, dark elves underground. Only two Old Norse texts mention it. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lfheimr)
14. **Swan maidens [FREE].** Tale type ATU 400, motif D361.1: a man steals a bathing woman's feather garment; she leaves when she regains it. Attested very widely; the oldest known narrative is Chinese, 4th century. Fits the existing avians only as a neighbour, not a copy. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_maiden)
15. **Hyperborea [FREE].** Herodotus 4.32-36: offerings pass tribe to tribe to Delos, first carried by named maidens. Later writers give lives of 1,000 years in happiness, Apollo wintering among them, and one sunrise a year. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperborea)
16. **Dilmun as pure land [FREE].** *Enki and Ninhursag*: predators do not kill, and there is no pain, disease, age or death; Enki promises fresh water. A real trading civilisation in the Gulf also bore the name. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilmun)

### Inferences
- Hooks: a people who live one season while outsiders live a century; a nation governed by nine co-ruling sisters; a court that repays every debt and insult exactly; a kingdom hidden beneath a false lake; a people bound to surrender a stolen garment or treasure.
- Gameplay colour could come from pledge and debt, not simply from "good" or "evil" fairies.
- A gift-chain nation (Hyperborea) suits a diplomacy-driven people who never meet their trading partners.

### Gaps
- The Oisín tale's date was not confirmed. I recall an 18th-century poem by Micheál Coimín, but the fetched page did not state this; check before citing.
- The fetched Seelie article gives the "Unseelie Court" as an 1819 coinage yet also attributes the description to Katharine Briggs; treat the history as unclear.
- The "tithe to hell" often attached to *Tam Lin* was not confirmed.

## Ashen Waste (dead world of ash and bone): which source motifs fit?

### Takeaway
Biblical desolation passages, 1 Enoch's giants and Norse world-burning give a precise vocabulary of ruin: salt, sulphur, smoke, hunger and bones. The key correction is that Genesis itself says very little about giants; almost all the detail is 1 Enoch.

### Cited Findings
1. **Sodom and Gomorrah [CARE].** Text, Gen 19:24-28: sulphur and fire fall; cities, plain and vegetation are overthrown; Lot's wife becomes a salt pillar; smoke rises like a furnace's. The word "ash" does not appear in my fetched summary. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+19:24-28&version=NRSVUE)
2. **Land burned to salt [CARE].** Text, Deut 29:23: a land burned out by sulphur and salt, unsown, nothing growing, likened to Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah and Zeboiim. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy+29:23&version=NRSVUE)
3. **Monument wasteland [FREE].** Text, Wisdom 10:6-7 (deuterocanonical): fire on the Five Cities; the land still smokes, its plants bear fruit that never ripens. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Wisdom+10:6-7&version=NRSVUE)
4. **Edom's burning pitch [CARE].** Text, Isa 34:9-15: streams become pitch and soil sulphur, burning day and night; owls, ravens, jackals, hyenas, goat-demons and Lilith take over its fortresses. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+34:9-15&version=NRSVUE)
5. **Valley of dry bones [CARE].** Text, Ezek 37:1-14: bones join, gain sinew, flesh, then breath, and stand as a multitude. God explains this as Israel's restoration, so it is a national hope, not a horror. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ezekiel+37:1-14&version=NRSVUE)
6. **Nephilim [FREE].** Text, Gen 6:1-4: "sons of God" take human wives; the Nephilim are present in those days and afterward. It never states outright that they are the offspring. Num 13:32-33 links Anakites to them, in a frightened spies' report. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+6:1-4;Numbers+13:32-33&version=NRSVUE)
7. **The Watchers' oath [FREE].** Text, 1 Enoch 6: 200 descend in Jared's days to Mount Hermon, led by Semjaza with 19 chiefs of tens, binding themselves by oath. — [1 Enoch online text](https://www.pseudepigrapha.com/pseudepigrapha/enoch.htm)
8. **Giants who devoured everything [FREE].** Text, 1 Enoch 7: giants of 3,000 ells consume all human goods, then humans, then sin against beasts and drink blood; the earth itself accuses them. — [1 Enoch online text](https://www.pseudepigrapha.com/pseudepigrapha/enoch.htm)
9. **Forbidden crafts [FREE].** Text, 1 Enoch 8: Azazel teaches swords, shields, metalwork, ornaments and cosmetics; others teach enchantment, roots, astrology and clouds. Good basis for a civilisation that burned through taught knowledge. — [1 Enoch online text](https://www.pseudepigrapha.com/pseudepigrapha/enoch.htm)
10. **Hungering spirits [FREE].** Text, 1 Enoch 15: spirits of the giants remain on earth, taking no food yet hungering and thirsting, and they afflict people until judgment. — [1 Enoch online text](https://www.pseudepigrapha.com/pseudepigrapha/enoch.htm)
11. **Azazel in the desert [FREE].** Text, Lev 16:8-10: lots over two goats; one is sent alive into the wilderness "for Azazel". 1 Enoch 10 is what makes him a bound being, cast into a rocky pit at Dudael, face covered. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+16:8-10&version=NRSVUE); [1 Enoch online text](https://www.pseudepigrapha.com/pseudepigrapha/enoch.htm)
12. **Rephaim, giants and shades [FREE].** Two senses: a tall people in Canaan (Deut 2; Og's iron bed of nine cubits, 3:11) and shades of the dead (Isa 14:9, 26:14; Job 26:5; Ps 88:10). Ugaritic *rpum* are deified royal ancestors given offerings. Scholars debate whether the senses share an origin. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rephaite); [Bible Gateway, Deut 3:11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy+3:11&version=KJV)
13. **Sheol [CARE].** Text: a still, dark underground realm for all the dead alike, about 66 occurrences, not moral punishment. Later: compartments and punishment emerge in the Second Temple period. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheol)
14. **Babel [CARE].** Text, Gen 11:1-9: people in Shinar build a city and tower to make a name; language is confused and they scatter. The text does not say the tower fell. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+11:1-9&version=NRSVUE)
15. **Mictlan's bones [CARE].** Most Aztec dead go there: nine levels, a four-year journey. Quetzalcoatl fetches bones from it, is tricked, and some break, explaining differing human heights. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mictlan)
16. **Ragnarök burning and survivors [FREE].** *Völuspá* and *Gylfaginning* 51-53: Surtr fires the world, the earth sinks, later rises green. Líf and Lífþrasir survive in Hoddmímis holt on dew. Fimbulwinter is three winters without summer. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragnar%C3%B6k)
17. **Iram of the Pillars [CARE].** Text, Qur'an 89:6-8: named beside 'Ad, Thamud and Pharaoh, unequalled in the lands, no location given. Later: city, tribe, Ubar and the Paradise of Shaddad are commentary and legend; the Ubar-at-Shisr identification is generally rejected. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iram_of_the_Pillars)
18. **Frashokereti's molten river [CARE].** Bundahishn (c. 9th century; the Avesta only alludes to it): metal of the mountains melts and all wade through it, warm milk to the righteous, burning to the wicked. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frashokereti)
19. **Arzareth, the refuge land [CARE].** Text, 2 Esdras 13:40-47: the tribes leave for an uninhabited land to keep their statutes; God stops the Euphrates; a journey of a year and a half. 2 Kings 17:6 gives only the Assyrian deportation. — [Bible Gateway, 2 Esdras](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Esdras+13:40-47&version=NRSVUE); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Lost_Tribes)

### Inferences
- Hooks: ancestor-kings who still receive offerings from their descendants; a people descended from crafts-teachers who blame their knowledge for the burning; pillar-builders whose ruined city has no agreed location; survivors living on dew in a sealed grove; a hungering people who cannot be fed; a people on a long march to a land empty of others, carrying a law.
- The existing machines and dwarves are the burned civilisation's obvious heirs, so new peoples could be its gravediggers, salt-miners, mourners, scavenger scholars and ash-farmers.
- Using the Sodom story, take the fire-and-salt image only. Avoid any echo of its moral or sexual reading, which is contested and could give offence.

### Gaps
- Ash as such: Num 19 and Gen 18:27 could add a ritual-ash theme, but these were not fetched.
- 1 Enoch translator: the page was fetched as an online text; I could not confirm from the fetch that it is R.H. Charles's translation, and sacred-texts.com returned 403.
- *Book of Giants* (Qumran and Manichaean) would suit this world but no detail was retrieved.
- Tradition that Babel's tower was destroyed by wind or fire is not confirmed here.

## Infernal Depths (lava, black glass underworld): which source motifs fit?

### Takeaway
Solomonic demon literature and Dante or Milton give an organised, urban, legalistic hell that is ideal for strategy-game societies. Greek and Norse fire-myths are free to use; Naraka, the Chinvat bridge and jinn belong to living faiths and should be abstracted.

### Cited Findings
1. **Gehenna, origin [CARE].** Text: the valley of Hinnom is a boundary (Josh 15:8; 18:16), then a site of child burning under Ahaz and Manasseh (2 Chr 28:3; 33:6); Jeremiah condemns Topheth (7:31-32) and Josiah defiles it (2 Kgs 23:10). Hell imagery is later. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gehenna)
2. **Gehenna, later forms [CARE].** Rabbinic: purification, often limited to 12 months. New Testament: warnings such as Mark 9:43. The "ever-burning rubbish dump" comes from David Kimhi (c. 1200) and has no direct evidence. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gehenna)
3. **Asmodeus in Tobit [FREE].** Text: he kills Sarah's seven husbands on their wedding nights (Wikipedia cites 6:13); the smell of burnt fish liver and heart drives him to the remotest parts of Egypt, where Raphael binds him hand and foot (8:2-3). — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Tobit+8:2-3&version=NRSVUE); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmodeus)
4. **Asmodeus, later [FREE].** Talmud: a humorous figure who is tricked into helping build the Temple and displaces Solomon for years. Later still: lust demon (*Malleus*), king of 72 legions (*Goetia*), a limping satirist (Lesage). He is demon throughout, so safe, but keep his name minor. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmodeus); [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/13843-solomon-testament-of)
5. **Testament of Solomon [FREE].** Greek pseudepigraphon: Michael gives a ring; demons are named, questioned and each assigned Temple labour: Ornias cuts stone, Beelzeboul saws marble, Onoskelis spins hemp, Asmodeus treads clay. — [Conybeare translation](http://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testament_of_Solomon)
6. **Each demon has its thwarting angel [FREE].** Bound spirits state their function and the angel or charm that defeats them; Ephippas the wind-demon is trapped in a flask. — [Conybeare translation](http://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm)
7. **Ars Goetia [FREE].** 72 titled spirits (king, duke, marquis, president, count, prince, knight); compiled mid-17th century, this portion no earlier than 1570, drawing chiefly on Weyer's *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars_Goetia)
8. **City of Dis [FREE].** Dante, *Inferno*: Styx ringed, iron-walled, guarded by fallen angels and Furies, holding lower Hell, with heretics in burning tombs. Its mosques reflect medieval hostility to Islam, so omit that detail. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dis_%28Divine_Comedy%29)
9. **Pandemonium [FREE].** Milton, *Paradise Lost* (1667) Book 1: the fallen dig ores under Mammon and smelt a palace out of the ground; Book 2 holds a formal debating council. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_Lost)
10. **Abaddon's pit [CARE].** Text, Rev 9:1-11: smoke from the bottomless pit releases scorpion-powered locusts told to spare plants; their king is Abaddon or Apollyon. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+9:1-11&version=NRSVUE)
11. **Tartarus [FREE].** Hesiod, *Theogony* 720-725: an anvil falls nine days from heaven to earth and nine more to Tartarus. Titans are chained there with the Hecatonchires as guards. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartarus)
12. **Typhon under the volcano [FREE].** Hesiod: hundred snake heads, fire, cast into Tartarus. Pindar and Apollodorus bury him under Etna, his struggles causing eruptions. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhon)
13. **Muspelheim [FREE].** *Gylfaginning*: Surtr guards it with a flaming sword; the sons of Muspell ride out in fire and Bifröst breaks under them. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muspelheim)
14. **Duat [FREE].** Twelve hour-regions and guarded gates; Ra's night voyage against Apep; lakes of fire, walls of iron and trees of turquoise; the heart weighed against Ma'at's feather, Ammit devouring failures. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duat)
15. **Naraka [CARE].** Hindu texts vary: 4, 7, 21 or 28 hells or more, under Yama, generally temporary before rebirth. Kumbhipaka boils in oil; Asipatravana has sword-leaved trees; Kalasutra is scorching copper. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naraka_%28Hinduism%29)
16. **Manichaean Realm of Darkness [FREE].** Five dark kingdoms mirror five light elements under a Prince of Darkness; evil beings swallowed light, and worlds are built from that mixture. Archons were hung over the heavens to recover it. Mani's religion faded out by the late 14th century. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manichaeism)
17. **Ahriman's counter-creation [CARE].** Vendidad 1: 16 good lands are each answered with a scourge. Gathas do not yet use the name; Bundahishn adds a spirit lacking foresight. Parsis now commonly hold a non-dualist view, so do not make him a game figure. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahriman)
18. **Chinvat Bridge [CARE].** Vendidad: two four-eyed dogs guard it. Bundahishn: wide for the righteous, narrow for the wicked, who are dragged to the House of Lies. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinvat_Bridge)
19. **Smokeless fire [CARE].** Qur'an 15:27, 55:15: jinn made of smokeless fire. Sura 72 has both righteous and malicious ones. Pre-Islamic Arabs placed them in desolate places; Pew 2012 found very high belief in Morocco and Bangladesh. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinn)

### Inferences
- Hooks: an infernal bureaucracy where every citizen has a declared function and a known weakness; a labour-bound people working off a ring-oath; a nation of ore-smelters who raised their capital from the ground; gatekeepers of an hour-by-hour twelve-gate road; a lightless people who hoard swallowed light; a volcano-priesthood who keep a buried giant asleep.
- Black glass has no strong source: treat it as invented colour tied to Muspell or the Duat's turquoise trees.
- Existing orcs and demons suit the warlike end, so new peoples should be administrative, mercantile, penitent or scholarly.

### Gaps
- Names of the five Manichaean dark kingdoms were not confirmed (I had smoke, fire, wind, water, darkness in mind but the fetch did not state them). Britannica was blocked (403).
- Testament of Solomon's date disagrees: 1st-3rd century in one article; 1st century to High Middle Ages in another. Accounts of Solomon's fall also differ (Shunammite versus Jebusite woman; different gods), likely due to recensions. His fall should not be used anyway, as Solomon is sacred in several religions.
- Buddhist Naraka and Milton's Mulciber were not confirmed by fetches.

## Plague Marshes (fetid bogs, dead forests): which source motifs fit?

### Takeaway
Mesopotamian disease demons and the Popol Vuh's named lords give the most usable societies of sickness, since each sickness has an office and a rival. Folklore adds the marsh setting itself.

### Cited Findings
1. **Plagues of Egypt [CARE].** Text: blood, frogs, gnats, flies, livestock death, boils from kiln soot, hail, locusts, three days' darkness, firstborn (Exod 7-12). Israelite livestock are spared (9:6). — [Bible Gateway, Exod 7-9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+7:20-21;Exodus+8:6;Exodus+8:17;Exodus+8:24;Exodus+9:6;Exodus+9:10;Exodus+9:23-25&version=NRSVUE); [KJV, Exod 10-12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+10:14-15;Exodus+10:22;Exodus+12:29&version=KJV)
2. **Worm bed [CARE].** Text, Isa 14:9-11: shades rise to meet a fallen ruler; worms are spread beneath him and cover him. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+14:9-11&version=KJV)
3. **Baal-zebub [CARE].** Text, 2 Kgs 1:2: the injured king Ahaziah sends to ask this god of Ekron whether he will recover, so he is consulted as an oracle on illness. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Kings+1:2&version=NRSVUE)
4. **Thirty-six decans [FREE].** Testament of Solomon: zodiac spirits each causing a single ailment, each with an angel or charm that repels it, such as "Lycourgos" written on ivy for a sore throat. — [Conybeare translation](http://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testament_of_Solomon)
5. **Lords of Xibalba [CARE].** *Popol Vuh* (K'iche'): paired lords hold offices, among them blood sickness, pus, jaundice, turning corpses to skeletons, stabbing from unswept corners, and death coughing blood on roads. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xibalba)
6. **Xibalba's road and houses [CARE].** Rivers of scorpions, blood and pus; Dark, Cold, Jaguar, Bat, Razor and Hot Houses. Nine layers is an interpretive gloss; the text focuses on landmarks. The tests kill or humiliate those who fail to outwit them. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xibalba); [Mayan.org, popular source](https://mayan.org/myths/xibalba-underworld/); [Hitchhiker's Guide to Religion, popular source](https://www.hitchhikersguidetoreligion.com/lore/popol-vuh-hero-twins/)
7. **Yomi [CARE].** *Kojiki*: having eaten underworld food, Izanami cannot simply leave. Izanagi lights a comb and sees her decay; thunder demons and foul women chase him; a boulder seals the way; washing purifies him. — [Wikipedia, Izanami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izanami); [Wikipedia, Yomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomi)
8. **Nergal and Erra [FREE].** Mesopotamian god of war, disease and death, worshipped into at least the Achaemenid period. In the *Epic of Erra* he rampages, urged by the Sibitti, until the attendant Ishum stops him. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nergal)
9. **Pazuzu [FREE].** Southwest wind-demon linked to plague, yet also a protective amulet against plague-winds and against Lamashtu. First text 7th century BC. Popularly known from *The Exorcist*, so a harmful plague-lord should use another name. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pazuzu)
10. **Lamashtu [FREE].** A baby-snatching demoness whom Pazuzu forces back to the underworld; amulets protected infants and pregnant women. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pazuzu)
11. **Hydra of Lerna [FREE].** Swamp near Lake Lerna, an underworld entrance; poisonous breath and blood; heads vary (nine in Alcaeus, fifty in Simonides); stumps are cauterised by Iolaus in Apollodorus. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lernaean_Hydra)
12. **Grendel's fen [FREE].** *Beowulf*: Grendel, descended from Cain, raids for twelve years and dies at his marsh den; his mother dwells in a mere. His appearance is never directly described. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grendel)
13. **Marsh lights [FREE].** Lights recede when approached. Stingy Jack and Will the Smith are refused by heaven and hell and wander with an ember. Marsh gas (phosphine, methane) is the leading explanation. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will-o%27-the-wisp)
14. **Nuckelavee [FREE].** Orkney: skinless horse-man from the sea whose breath withers crops; cannot cross fresh running water. Kelp-burning smoke enraged it, bringing the horse plague mortasheen. Source is Dennison (published 1880-94), who may have romanticised it. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuckelavee)
15. **Nasu [CARE].** Zoroastrian corpse-demon in fly form; touching a corpse spreads contamination; a dog's gaze drives her out. Linked to Towers of Silence. Zoroastrianism is a living religion, so use only the idea of contagious pollution. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasu_%28Zoroastrianism%29)
16. **Filth river and worm-lake [CARE].** Hindu hells: Vaitarani, a river of filth at Naraka's boundary, and Krimibhojana, a lake of worms for those who do not share food. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naraka_%28Hinduism%29)
17. **Fungal castaways [FREE].** Hodgson, "The Voice in the Night" (November 1907): a shipwrecked couple eat an island's fungus. LibriVox offers a public-domain audiobook, though I found no formal copyright statement. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Voice_in_the_Night_%28short_story%29)

### Inferences
- Hooks: a marsh college whose every physician is sworn to one disease and carries its charm; twin-ruled death-courts with offices and trials of wit; a mercantile people of protection-amulets who are both cause and cure; ember-carriers refused by every realm; a culture that keeps a corpse-watching animal; bog folk who forbid running water.
- Avoid repeating the existing ratkin (plague, swarm) and myconids (fungus): give new peoples frogs, leeches, reeds, eels, wisps, herons, peat and bog preservation.
- Disease content should remain mythic; do not depict real illness groups or stigmatise.

### Gaps
- "Lord of flies" as the meaning of Baal-zebub, and Beelzebul's New Testament role, were not verified in fetches.
- Bog bodies and marsh-dweller cultures were not researched; if used, they need separate checks.
- Modern Maya communities are not discussed in the fetched article; from my general knowledge K'iche' and other Maya peoples are living communities, hence the [CARE] tag.

## Drowned Deep (endless ocean, islands, coral): which source motifs fit?

### Takeaway
Near-Eastern sea monsters, drowned-city legends and fish-sages cover this world well. The strongest original idea is the Apkallu: wise fish-cloaked teachers from before the flood, far less familiar than Atlantis.

### Cited Findings
1. **The Flood [CARE].** Text, Gen 7:11, 7:24: fountains of the deep burst and windows of heaven open; the waters prevail 150 days. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+7:11;Genesis+7:24&version=KJV)
2. **Leviathan in scripture [CARE].** Text: Ps 74:13-14 breaks his "heads" (plural, no number) and gives him as food; Isa 27:1 calls him a crooked serpent to be slain; Job 41:18-21 gives sparks, smoke and flame. Job 40:15-19 has Behemoth, grass-eating with bones like iron. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+74:13-14;Isaiah+27:1;Job+41:18-21;Job+40:15-19&version=KJV)
3. **Leviathan in apocalyptic [FREE].** 1 Enoch 60:7-9: female Leviathan in the depths, male Behemoth in a wilderness. 2 Esdras 6:49-52: divided because waters could not hold both; Leviathan gets the watery seventh part. 2 Baruch 29:4: food for the elect. — [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/9841-leviathan); [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Esdras+6:49-52&version=NRSVUE)
4. **Leviathan's hide [CARE].** Rabbinic (Baba Batra 74a-75a): the female was slain so the species would not overrun the world; hide becomes tents and girdles; banquet flesh for the righteous. Many commentators, such as Maimonides, read this allegorically. — [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/9841-leviathan)
5. **Tiamat [FREE].** *Enūma Eliš*: salt sea paired with Abzû; she makes eleven monsters and gives Qingu the Tablet of Destinies; Marduk splits her into heaven and earth. Features include breasts, tail and udder. No confident dragon form; the multi-headed dragon comes from 1970s D&D. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiamat)
6. **Atlantis [FREE].** Plato's *Critias*: Poseidon rings Cleito's hill with land and water; five pairs of twins; ten kings. Orichalcum, elephants, 10,000 chariots and 1,200 ships. Classicists generally consider it fiction. — [Jowett, MIT Classics](http://classics.mit.edu/Plato/critias.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis)
7. **Atlantean law and decline [FREE].** Kings meet at Poseidon's temple, hunt a bull, sacrifice it over a law-inscribed pillar, and judge at night in azure robes. Divine nature fades and greed grows; Zeus gathers the gods and the text breaks off. The sinking in a day and night comes from elsewhere in the account. — [Jowett, MIT Classics](http://classics.mit.edu/Plato/critias.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis)
8. **Ys [FREE].** Breton legend, recorded late 15th to 17th centuries: city on reclaimed land behind a dike, king holds the key; Dahut steals it and the sea enters. Dahut is first known in Le Grand. Bells are heard at low tide. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ys)
9. **Kitezh [CARE].** Russian: the city sinks into Lake Svetloyar in answer to prayer when Batu's army comes; bells heard; the pure may find it. First reference is the anonymous late 18th-century *Kitezh Chronicle*, thought to be Old Believer. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitezh)
10. **Dvaraka [CARE].** Mahabharata, Mausala Parva: the sea breaks its bounds and floods Krishna's city as Arjuna watches. A Sapta Puri pilgrimage city today, and underwater archaeological claims are contested. Use only "a city the sea took back". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dv%C4%81rak%C4%81)
11. **Nāgas [CARE].** Half-human serpents of the netherworld Patala, full of gems they guard, tied to water; eternal rivals of Garuda. Mucalinda shelters the Buddha. Khmer people claim naga descent; Thailand made the naga a national symbol in 2022. Do not use the name. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C4%81ga)
12. **Apkallu and Oannes [FREE].** Seven sages from before the flood, shown as fish-cloaked men. Berossus has fish-men teach humans, the first being Oannes. After the flood sages are human *ummanu*. *Poem of Erra* banishes them to the Apsu. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apkallu)
13. **Dagon's fallen idol [FREE].** Text, 1 Sam 5:1-5: his image falls before the ark twice, head and hands severed on the threshold, which priests thereafter do not tread. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Samuel+5:1-5&version=NRSVUE)
14. **Telchines [FREE].** Rhodes: flippered, dog-headed "fish children" (Eustathius); metalworkers who forged Poseidon's trident and Cronus's sickle; destructive gaze, poison from Stygian water and sulphur; destroyed by the gods, in some versions by flood. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telchines)
15. **Rán [FREE].** Norse sea goddess with a net, catching sailors; wife of Ægir, with nine wave-daughters. Her name means plunder. Simek sees her ruling the drowned dead on the seabed. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A1n)
16. **Dragon palace and time slip [CARE].** Urashima visits Ryūgū-jō and returns about 300 years later. Early *otogizōshi* place him on Hōrai instead; the *Kojiki* has a fish-scale palace. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ry%C5%ABg%C5%AB-j%C5%8D)
17. **Abezethibou [FREE].** Testament of Solomon: Red Sea demon who claims he hardened Pharaoh's heart; bound to hold up a pillar until the world's end. — [Conybeare translation](http://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm)
18. **Naglfar [FREE].** Prose Edda: a ship made of dead men's nails, steered by Hrym at Ragnarök. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragnar%C3%B6k)
19. **Ziusudra's island [FREE].** Some versions of the Eridu Genesis place the flood hero in Dilmun, living forever. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilmun)

### Inferences
- Hooks: fish-cloaked sage guilds that wear their learning; dike-keepers who keep one key; rival coral kingdoms of ten kings with a bull-free oath ritual (substituting something oceanic); bell-ringers of a city that sank willingly; net-wardens who claim all drowned property; pillar-bearers holding up a sunken structure forever; shipwrights of a gruesome material; hide-tent nomads living on a slain sea-giant.
- Existing merfolk and lizardfolk occupy swim and shore, so new peoples can be raft-dwellers, coral masons, whale-followers, storm-divers and island hermits.
- Using Atlantis directly is overdone; Ys, Telchines and the Apkallu feel fresher.

### Gaps
- Stephanus numbers for *Critias* were not visible in the fetched translation; the Perseus link failed. I recall the account running from about 113c to its break at 121c; verify before citing.
- The fish form of Dagon is a later claim not confirmed here; 1 Samuel as fetched does not state it. Lovecraft's "Father Dagon" should not be echoed.
- Seven-headed Leviathan is not stated in Ps 74 (only "heads"); its source (Ugaritic) was not fetched.
- Gilgamesh Tablet XI and Lyonesse were not checked.

## Which popular claims are later tradition rather than text (biblical and mythic audit)?

### Takeaway
Most vivid details people "remember" are later additions. Use this list to keep invented lore from falsely claiming scriptural authority.

### Cited Findings
- Giants' height (3,000 ells), their cannibalism and the 200 Watchers are in 1 Enoch 6-7, not Genesis, which only names the Nephilim in four verses. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+6:1-4&version=NRSVUE); [1 Enoch online text](https://www.pseudepigrapha.com/pseudepigrapha/enoch.htm)
- "Watchers" is not a Genesis term; linking "sons of God" to Watchers is interpretation. Aramaic *ʿirin* becomes "angel" in Greek and Ethiopic versions. 2 Enoch's Grigori are a separate, later tradition. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watcher_%28angel%29)
- Asmodeus: the Dead Sea Scroll fragments of Tobit call him only "demon". His fame for lust, legions and Solomon's throne is later. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmodeus)
- Solomon's bound spirits are not in Kings or Chronicles as far as these sources show; the seal-ring comes from Gittin 68a-b, the Testament and Arabic expansions (four jewels, the ring in a fish), with Josephus reporting an exorcism by a Solomonic ring. — [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/13843-solomon-testament-of)
- Gehenna as a rubbish fire is medieval conjecture. Sheol is not a place of punishment. — [Wikipedia, Gehenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gehenna); [Wikipedia, Sheol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheol)
- Ten Lost Tribes: only about a fifth of the northern population may have been moved (Finkelstein's estimate, about 40,000). The Sambation river, Red Jews, American and British descent theories are later; British Israelism and similar claims lack support, and Tudor Parfitt describes the myth as part of colonial discourse. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Lost_Tribes)
- 2 Esdras manuscripts vary between nine, nine and a half and ten tribes. — [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2+Esdras+13:40-47&version=NRSVUE)
- Iram: the Qur'an gives no location. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iram_of_the_Pillars)
- Ahriman: not a proper name in the Gathas; daevas there are "wrong gods" rather than demons. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahriman)
- Chinvat: a Zoroastrian priest-researcher, Cyrus Niknam, argues the bridge idea came through outside influence in the Sasanian era; scholars also dispute its form. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinvat_Bridge)
- Tiamat as a multi-headed dragon derives from D&D, not the text. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiamat)
- Avalon first appears around 1136 and Glastonbury's claim is later still. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalon)
- Xibalba's nine layers are not detailed in the Popol Vuh, and sources disagree on the number of trial houses. — [Hitchhiker's Guide to Religion, popular source](https://www.hitchhikersguidetoreligion.com/lore/popol-vuh-hero-twins/); [Mayan.org, popular source](https://mayan.org/myths/xibalba-underworld/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xibalba)
- Kitezh's dating fails: the supposed founder was born in 1189, after Gorodets's founding in 1152. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitezh)

### Inferences
- In-game text should say "the old tales" rather than quote scripture, and should use invented names. This protects both accuracy and respect.

### Gaps
- 1 Enoch dating (some scholars give 2nd-1st century BC for the Watchers section) was seen only in a Wikipedia summary, and no consensus is stated. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watcher_%28angel%29)

## Which science-fiction and fantasy tropes are usable, and which names must not be used?

### Takeaway
All the tropes themselves are generic and usable. Protection attaches to specific names, characters and expressions, so use the idea with new names and avoid the list below. This is not legal advice.

### Cited Findings
- **Generation ships.** Goddard's "interstellar ark" (written 1918), Tsiolkovsky's multigenerational "Noah's Ark" (1928), Bernal's globes (1929). Heinlein's *Orphans of the Sky* and Aldiss's *Non-Stop* established social breakdown aboard. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_ship)
- **Post-apocalyptic monks.** Walter M. Miller Jr., *A Canticle for Leibowitz* (1959): after nuclear war, the Simplification kills the learned; a Catholic order preserves fragments for over a thousand years. Do not use Leibowitz or "Simplification". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Canticle_for_Leibowitz)
- **Machine cults.** Forster, "The Machine Stops" (1909): "Mechanism" worships the Machine; rejecters face Homelessness; it collapses. Butler, *Erewhon* (1872), "The Book of the Machines": machines evolve by selection. Dune's "Butlerian Jihad" draws on this; avoid that term. — [Wikipedia, Forster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Machine_Stops); [Wikipedia, Erewhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erewhon)
- **Hive minds.** Wells, *The First Men in the Moon* (1901); Keller, *The Human Termites* (1929); Stapledon, *Last and First Men* (1930), first known "group mind"; "hive mind" from Hamilton (1942) and Schmitz (1950). Do not use Borg or Zerg. Vinge's Tines model a decentralised alternative. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hive_mind_%28science_fiction%29)
- **Uplifted animals.** Wells, *The Island of Doctor Moreau* (1896); Kafka, "A Report to an Academy" (1917); de Camp's "Johnny Black"; Cordwainer Smith's "underpeople"; Brin's *Uplift* popularised the term. Avoid "Instrumentality" and "underpeople", and Brin's specific races. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uplift_%28science_fiction%29)
- **Terraformers.** Williamson coined "terraform" in "Collision Orbit" (1942); Wells's Martians as xenoformers (1898); Clarke's *The Sands of Mars* (1951); Herbert's Fremen (1965); Robinson's *Mars Trilogy*. Avoid Fremen and Arrakis. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terraforming_in_fiction)
- **Fungal networks.** Hodgson's 1907 story; its adaptation *Matango* (1963); Lumley's "Fruiting Bodies". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Voice_in_the_Night_%28short_story%29)
- **Sunken cities.** Lovecraft's Deep Ones: hybrids, undersea city Y'ha-nthlei, Esoteric Order of Dagon. Avoid these names as a cautious choice, since the fetched page does not state copyright status. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_One)
- **Warhammer.** A third-party list of Games Workshop registrations includes Adeptus Mechanicus and Tyranid. Skaven are Games Workshop's rat race. The company has pursued even "space marine" against an e-book, though critics disputed that claim. — [MyMiniFactory](https://creator.myminifactory.com/registered-trademarks-from-games-workshop); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skaven); [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/space-marines-warhammer-ultramarines-hogarth-games-workshop-432520)
- **Dungeons & Dragons.** The OGL Product Identity list named beholder, gauth, carrion crawler, tanar'ri, baatezu, displacer beast, githyanki, githzerai, mind flayer, illithid, umber hulk and yuan-ti. Commentators note it covers the named creature, not the broad concept. Sourced from forums, so check the licence text. — [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/srd-where-is-the-beholder.128491); [Prismatic Wasteland](https://www.prismaticwasteland.com/blog/no-one-owns-these-monsters)
- *The Mists of Avalon* made "Lady of the Lake" a priestess title; do not copy that framing. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_of_the_Lake)

### Inferences
- The sealed-worlds setting gains from "sealed-ark" and "keepers of fragments" ideas without any named borrowing, for example an Ashen Waste order preserving the burned civilisation's fragments, or a Drowned Deep raft-city that has forgotten it is a vessel.
- Check every invented name by search before release. "Ratmen" and "fungus-folk" as descriptions are generic; "Skaven" is not.

### Gaps
- Additional names I would also avoid from memory but did not verify: Mi-Go and other Cthulhu Mythos coinages, *Nausicaä*'s toxic jungle, Warhammer's Chaos gods, Elder Scrolls and Warcraft race names, Tolkien coinages. The search for Mi-Go and Nausicaä returned nothing.
- Lovecraft's copyright status was not established. Trademark checks need a real registry search, and ideally a lawyer before commercial release. The Skaven trademark itself was not confirmed.

## How do reputable games and writers handle living religions, and what should be avoided?

### Takeaway
The recorded pattern favours dead traditions, softened treatment, disclaimers and invented faiths. Complaints arose chiefly from sacred figures being playable or sexualised, sacred sites being battlegrounds and scripture being used carelessly. Fifty invented peoples drawing on demons, apocrypha and legend sit well clear of this.

### Cited Findings
- Games often draw on no-longer-practised religions, such as ancient Greek, and tend to soften living ones when used. Invented faiths exist too (Morrowind's Tribunal Temple, Skyrim's Talos). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_and_video_games)
- *Assassin's Creed* opens by stating it was made by a multicultural team of various faiths and beliefs. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_and_video_games); [Steam discussion, weak source](https://steamcommunity.com/app/236870/discussions/0/1621724915797485017)
- *SMITE* (2012): Rajan Zed of the Universal Society of Hinduism asked Hi-Rez to remove Agni, Kali and Vamana, playable as fighters; Hi-Rez declined but later changed a Kali website image, and the Hindu American Foundation was also in talks. Critics question whether Zed speaks for all Hindus. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/hindu-leader-requests-removal-of-dieties-from-smite-hi-rez-declines/); [CinemaBlend](https://cinemablend.com/games/Hi-Rez-Changes-Porno-Style-Image-Kali-SMITE-Website-45103.html); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2012-07-31-hindu-group-continues-to-protest-smite-asks-quakecon-to-pull-th.html)
- *Hitman 2* (2002): a level in a Harmandir Sahib-like temple drew Sikh Coalition complaints, and Eidos amended the game. — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/eidos-responds-to-sikh-controversy/1100-2897279); [Pluralism Project](https://pluralism.org/news/eidos-agrees-remove-offensive-content-depicting-sikhs-0)
- *LittleBigPlanet* (2008): Sony recalled and re-pressed the game worldwide because a licensed song contained Qur'anic lines. *Ocarina of Time*'s chant was similarly removed in later versions. — [MTV](https://www.mtv.com/news/wntrxa/littlebigplanet-release-delayed-over-quotes-from-quran); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_and_video_games)
- Scholar Souvik Mukherjee criticises *Far Cry 4* for reducing Himalayan beliefs to exotic atmosphere and combat objectives. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_and_video_games)
- Scholarship: Campbell and Grieve co-edited a 2014 volume, which I recall as *Playing with Religion in Digital Games*; the fetched summary worded the title slightly differently, so check it. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_and_video_games)
- Indigenous peoples can object even to historical leaders: a Cree Chief Headman objected that *Civilization VI* portrayed Poundmaker's people as colonial conquerors. — [CinemaBlend](https://cinemablend.com/games/1769099/why-one-poundmaker-cree-nation-headman-is-upset-with-civilization-vi)
- Attitudes toward depicting Muhammad vary more than is often portrayed, but the point is avoided by leaving him out. Civ VI's Arabia is led by Saladin. — [Mark Pack](https://www.markpack.org.uk/117473/us-supreme-court-includes-depiction-muhammad/); [Gameranx](https://gameranx.com/updates/id/75967/article/meet-the-civilization-vi-leader-of-arabia/)
- Living practice is real in these sources: jinn belief (Pew 2012), Dvārakā pilgrimage, Thai and Khmer naga traditions. — [Wikipedia, Jinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinn); [Wikipedia, Dvārakā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dv%C4%81rak%C4%81); [Wikipedia, Nāga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C4%81ga)

### Inferences
My recommendation, drawn from the incidents above rather than any published standard:
- **Never as leaders, villains, or playable or monstrous figures:** God under any name; Jesus, Mary, Muhammad and his family, Moses, Abraham, Noah as central prophets, Solomon as a character; archangels Michael, Gabriel, Raphael; Buddha; Krishna, Vishnu, Shiva, Kali, Hanuman, Shesha, Vasuki; Amaterasu, Izanami, Izanagi; Ahura Mazda, Zoroaster; Sikh Gurus; Hud.
- **Do not make villains of whole real peoples or faiths:** no invented people should map onto Jews, Muslims, Hindus, Parsis, Sikhs, Maya, Nahua or any other living group. The Lost Tribes are especially risky: use an invented "long-marching people", never a descent claim.
- **Avoid real sacred places as settings or enemy forts:** Hinnom/Jerusalem, Dwarka, Mecca, Harmandir Sahib, Ise, Lake Svetloyar as such. Use imagery instead.
- **No scripture in text, audio or chants.** Vet licensed music too.
- **Safe wells:** Testament of Solomon and Goetia demons, 1 Enoch's fallen Watchers (with renaming), Mesopotamian, Greek, Norse, Egyptian, Irish, Welsh, Manichaean material, and literature such as Dante, Milton and Beowulf. 1 Enoch is canonical in Ethiopian Orthodox tradition from my recollection (unverified here), so rename its beings too.
- **Use new names throughout.** "Inspired by" is invisible to players, and original names both meet the owner's brief and remove most of the risk.
- **Consider a brief disclaimer** in the credits, Assassin's Creed style, once a real team statement can honestly be made.
- **Separately,** the game's existing 23 real faiths system (`Religion.cs`) is outside these notes but raises the same questions, so it may merit its own review.

### Gaps
- I found no published formal guideline for religious representation in games, and no official Firaxis statement on why prophets are omitted as leaders; a CivFanatics thread relaying a claim that Muhammad was once considered for Civ IV is unconfirmed. — [CivFanatics](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/muhammad-was-going-to-be-an-arabian-leader-in-civ-iv.689719/)
- Sensitivity readers from relevant communities would be better than any desk research; none were consulted.
- Source limits for the whole document: sacred-texts.com, Britannica and Perseus were blocked or failed, so many mythology entries rely on Wikipedia, and pages were read through a summarising tool, not line by line. Bible Gateway, Conybeare, the MIT Jowett text and the Jewish Encyclopedia were the nearest primary or reputable sources reached. Verse and stanza numbers quoted via Wikipedia should be checked before appearing in anything published.
