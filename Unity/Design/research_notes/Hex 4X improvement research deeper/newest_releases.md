## What shipped in 2025-2026, and what worked or failed?

### Takeaway
The biggest strategy launches of the period spent 2026 repairing themselves, and the repairs were mostly clarity, performance and player freedom rather than new systems. Civ VII's largest update doubled its player count for a while but barely moved its rating. Two big Paradox updates (Stellaris 4.0, EU5 1.2) drew backlash for instability. The cleanest successes were long, open early-access runs with frequent updates (Endless Legend 2, Terra Invicta, Against the Storm).

### Inferences
- **Clarity work dominates what large studios fixed after launch.** Several cheap ideas translate generally, and none needs copying any game's content:
  - a range overlay that follows the cursor (Paradigm's city reach of 2-5 tiles would suit one);
  - a panel naming who caused each change in a relationship;
  - closing a negotiation screen to look at the map;
  - tooltips that never cover the tile being inspected;
  - a setting to switch each victory type on or off.
  - I have not checked which of these Paradigm Realm already has.
- **Zone of control and melee retaliation were added late to a hex 4X** to stop units slipping past lines. It is worth checking how Paradigm's hex combat behaves before raiders arrive.
- **Costs that scale per building type rather than globally** are one team's answer to district spam. Paradigm's specialists and buildings could be checked against the same pattern if play shows spam.
- **Event presentation matters as much as the event count.** Amplitude's fix was art plus fewer clicks, which adds a practical note to the first round's event-card recommendation.
- **Large system updates are the main reputational risk.** Stellaris 4.0 and EU5 1.2 suggest a rule for Paradigm: after any systems change, measure late-game turn time and load an old save before calling it finished. The harnesses already allow this.
- **The Civ VII result gives a limit.** A large free fix can bring players back for a while without changing the overall rating, so first impressions are hard to repair.

## What do small-team 4X developers say about scope, what they cut, and what players rewarded?

### Takeaway
The clearest shared message is that small teams survive by cutting hard and having a rule for what is in. The clearest warning comes from a solo developer who nearly burned out from over-ambition. Evidence comes from a handful of interviews, so it shows what developers believe, not what is proven.

### Cited Findings
- **Shadow Empire (solo):**
  - "I was way too ambitious designing Shadow Empire." It ran years over plan and nearly caused burnout.
  - He designed Private Economy and Militia so players can neglect those systems while learning.
  - He wants a simulation feel for immersion without scientific accuracy.
  - He credits word of mouth, reviews and streamers for reaching a wider audience than his earlier series.
  - He answers criticism of the graphics by arguing the look will age well. [READ, April 2022] — [Matchstick Eyes](https://matchstickeyes.com/2022/04/03/shadow-empire-interview-with-victor-reijkersz)
- **Polytopia (team of six):**
  - Cut "unnecessary systems" to focus on 4X core elements, hid some information in menus to keep the interface clean, and "avoided including a lengthy tutorial", teaching through play.
  - New features go to the internal team, then a small group of hardcore players.
  - "More than 20 million downloads" is the article's introduction, not the CEO. [READ, 24 October 2025] — [Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/features/interviews/interview-with-midjiwan-ceo-christian-lovstedt-on-the-battle-of-polytopia)
- **Polytopia's creator:** the team "kept simplifying and removing stuff until they reached the core", and he finds it "fascinating" how often a fun idea becomes a less fun game. [READ, updated 20 February 2025] — [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/the-battle-of-polytopia/interview)
- **Conflict:** the share of ideas scrapped is about 80% in Pocket Tactics and about 90% in Cubed3. Different speakers and dates, so read it as "most".
- **Unciv (volunteer, open source; 11.5k GitHub stars):** a blunt inclusion rule, "If it's in the original Civ V, then yes!" Anything else goes to modding, not the base game. On a Civ VI version: "Considering how long it took to get this far, no." Steam declined to host it. [READ] — [GitHub](https://www.github.com/yairm210/Unciv)
- **Ozymandias:** its Steam page says tedious management was cut and combat simplified to a Power system where players only move armies and fleets; single-player turns offer several opportunities of which you pick one. [READ] — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1768280)
- **Dune: Spice Wars (Shiro):** the first-year article says its tweaks were "almost entirely" suggested by players, including military positioning, combat notifications, map readability, flexible multiplayer and a colour-blind mode. Balancing is aimed at no faction gaining "total supremacy". No cuts are mentioned. [READ, 10 July 2023; publisher's site] — [Level Infinite](https://www.levelinfinite.com/news/dune-spice-wars-year-one-and-the-road-ahead/)
- **Before We Leave:** began as one person's hobby project alongside full-time work and fatherhood. Sales funded an office and permanent hires. Players objected to the "non-violent" description because of laser cannons and planet-eating whales, and the studio dropped it. [SNIPPET] — [Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/society/23-03-2022/inside-the-dunedin-studio-making-a-nice-video-game); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_We_Leave)
- **Dominions 6:** Wargamer's verdict centres on the detailed battle simulator as "the beating heart", and Illwinter's own notes talk about tuning armies against magic. Its appeal is depth in one thing, not breadth. [SNIPPET] — [Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/dominions-6/review); [Illwinter](https://illwinter.com/dom6/changes.html)
- **Songs of Syx:** the one-person developer names onboarding, not features, as the biggest current problem, while still holding a high rating (see the Steam table). [READ] — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1162750/Songs_of_Syx/)
- **Update habits:** EL2's nearly five updates a month and Against the Storm's two-weekly rhythm are the two explicit cadences. [READ] — [Amplitude](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/endless-legend-2/blogs/1084-road-to-1-0-early-access-retrospective); [SNIPPET] — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/city-builder/one-of-our-favorite-city-builders-has-sold-over-a-million-copies-on-steam-and-theres-an-expansion-on-the-way)

### Inferences
- **Paradigm Realm's risk is the Reijkersz pattern.** The project now has 180 technologies, 22 civilisations, 50 sealed peoples, 23 faiths and five locked worlds, while no human has played a hex game by hand. It would help to write a one-line inclusion rule like Unciv's. Mine would be: "nothing new until the last feature has been played by hand." The owner should choose the wording.
- **Reijkersz's idea of systems that can safely be ignored while learning** fits the existing Governor and worker auto-develop. It is a way to reduce first-game load without removing anything.
- **Coherent art rather than high-end art seems enough.** Songs of Syx is a solo pixel-art game with a high rating, and Shadow Empire spread by word of mouth despite graphics criticism. This is thin evidence, and EL2's commenters show that players do notice art direction.
- **Announcing what came from players** is a free habit if the game goes public. Not relevant until it has players.

## Why did short-form 4X work?

### Takeaway
Short games appear to work when length is designed in from the start by removing whole kinds of management, and when one game is small enough to replay. The evidence does not show that short is better: long, dense games such as Terra Invicta are also well rated, and Civ VII's attempt to restructure a long game around Ages was rolled back in part.

### Cited Findings
- **Polytopia:** Lövstedt says faster games are "easier to pick up and play in short bursts", which suits mobile. Ekenstam wanted a faster Civ-style game because later entries had slowed. The interview does not explain the 30-turn figure. [READ] — [Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/features/interviews/interview-with-midjiwan-ceo-christian-lovstedt-on-the-battle-of-polytopia). Third-party sources describe a 30-turn score mode, and the original release had only that mode. [SNIPPET] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battle_of_Polytopia)
- **Ozymandias:** "Build an empire in a single sitting", reached by cutting management and tactical combat. [READ] — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1768280)
- **Conflict on Ozymandias:**
  - An Xbox article (18 December 2025) lists "tactical city placement and army management" and says the game suits both short sessions and longer showdowns. It gives no length. [READ, promotional] — [TheXboxHub](https://www.thexboxhub.com/ozymandias-arrives-on-xbox-a-sharper-smarter-take-on-4x-strategy/)
  - GOG's 41.5-hour figure appears to be a community estimate and does not describe one match. [SNIPPET] — [GOG](https://www.gog.com/game/ozymandias_bronze_age_empire_sim)
- **Hexarchy:** a hex 4X whose games "last less than an hour", built on deckbuilding from researched technologies and adopted civics. Its overall rating is 83%, but recent reviews are 71% of only 14. [READ; WEAK] — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1356810/Hexarchy/)
- **Against the Storm** is the same logic outside 4X: cities are not meant to last, and a new map comes each cycle. It has now sold 2 million copies. [SNIPPET on design] — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/against-the-storm/steam-sales); [READ on sales] — [WorthPlaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2026/8/12/news/150690-against-the-storm-surpasses-2-million-copies-sold-across-all-platforms/)
- **Civ VII's Ages:** the first round covers the intent. In this round, update 1.4.1 removed a Crisis from one Age, and 1.5.0 changed resource spawning so that some victories are less frustrating. Firaxis is still tuning the structure 19 months after launch. - **Counter-example, long games:** Terra Invicta's 1.0 sits at about 82-87% despite warnings it takes dozens of hours. [SNIPPET; WEAK] — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/New-grand-strategy-game-launches-on-Steam-with-promising-reviews-and-35-launch-discount.1197887.0.html). ### Inferences
- **Three shared traits, my reading:** whole chores are removed rather than sped up; the length is stated plainly on the box; and a lost game costs little, so replaying is natural. Old World's turn limit (first round) shares the last two.
- **Polytopia's reasoning is tied to mobile,** so it may not carry over fully to a Windows game.
- **Shortening a game after the fact looks harder than designing it short.** Civ VII's experience is consistent with this, though one game cannot prove it.
- **For Paradigm Realm:** the first round's advice to keep the roughly 230-turn length and fix the ending still stands. A cheap addition to consider is a "quick game" preset (Tiny or Small, few rivals, one early-finish rule, no sealed worlds). It would give new players a first game they can finish and would give the owner a faster way to play-test by hand. This is my proposal, untested, and a true short game would need chores removed rather than a mere turn cap.

### Gaps
- No studio here publishes completion or retention data by game length.
- I found no developer statement on why 30 turns specifically, and no designer interview for Ozymandias.
- Average real-time length of a Polytopia or Ozymandias match is unsourced beyond marketing lines.
