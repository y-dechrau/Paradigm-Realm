# Review of the 40 sealed peoples: name clashes, accuracy, respect, consistency

Report only; no game file was edited. Not legal advice: the name grades below are a desk search for confusion and courtesy, not a trademark clearance. No trademark registry was searched directly.

Check labels used throughout:
- **read** = page fetched, but read through a summarising tool that returns short extracts, not line by line.
- **snippet** = search-result text only.
- **secondary** = reference work, encyclopedia or Wikipedia, including items carried over from `civilisation_inspirations.md` ("the notes").
- **local** = read in full from the project's own source files.
- **memory** = my own recollection, not checked; treat as a lead.

## What is the verdict on each people?

### Takeaway
No people depicts a figure sacred to a living faith, and none maps openly onto a real religious or ethnic group. Nothing sexualised was found. One name is a close clash (the unit "Ravener"). One story needs rewriting: the Stiltwind Factors stack up the medieval plague-and-debt slander. Two need lighter edits (The Manifest, Flensers of Ummoth). Most of the rest pass; several source comments need correcting.

### Inferences
- Only "Ravener" is close enough that I would change it regardless. It is a product name in the same genre from a company known for enforcing.
- Everything graded weak shares an ordinary word, which is hard to monopolise but worth a real search before any commercial release.
- `SealedPeoples.cs` line 7-8 claims "no name comes from anyone's fiction". This review shows that cannot be stated flatly, so the comment should be softened.
- Beyond the brief, existing entries deserve the same pass. Entry 38 (Scaled Dominion) has "Coatl Priest", stepped pyramids, star priests and the epithet "Old Ones": this is Mesoamerican-coded and close to Warhammer's Lizardmen (memory). The catalogue also holds a real Maya civilisation. "Skritt" (entry 35) is, from memory, a rat people in Guild Wars 2. These are not among the 40 but the sealed-world histories lean on them.

### Gaps
- Not searched: nearly all leader, unit, building and city names, and the three SF "Acting" titles. No registry search (USPTO, EUIPO, UKIPO) was run directly.
- Combined OR queries often ignored quotation marks. A clean pass would search each name alone on BoardGameGeek, Steam, itch.io, DriveThruRPG and Goodreads, and a lawyer should clear names before commercial release.

## Are the motif claims accurate, and do comments overstate their sources?

### Takeaway
Only `SealedPeoplesAshenWaste.cs` and `SealedPeoplesPlagueMarshes.cs` have per-people inspiration comments. Fae Wilds, Infernal Depths and Drowned Deep have none, so nothing there overstates, but nothing is recorded either. Of the 14 comments that exist, four need correcting (Ivy Collegium, Salt Vigil, Paired Assize, Seated Kings). One error in the notes is confirmed: the ivy charm is not in the Testament of Solomon.

### Cited Findings

**Comment checks**
- **Ivy Collegium (overstates).** Comment: each spirit "turned back by its own charm". In Conybeare, about 15 decans yield to a spoken angel or divine name, about 13 to something written or worn, about 6 to other remedies. The numbered text gives 34, the fourth and twenty-second missing, though it opens with "the thirty-six elements" — [Conybeare, read](http://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm). The counts are the tool's approximation.
- **Correction to the notes (Plague Marshes item 4).** The word "ivy" does not occur. The charm is "write on fig-leaves 'Lycurgos'", for the decan Agchonion, with no ailment named — [Conybeare, read](http://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm). The Collegium's ivy is therefore pure invention. That is fine in play, but must not be attributed. My direct download of the page failed, so this rests on the summarising tool; check by eye before citing.
- **Unfed (accurate).** 200 descending on Hermon, swearing together, giants of "three thousand ells", Azazel teaching swords, spirits who "take no food, but nevertheless hunger and thirst" (15:11) — [1 Enoch online text, read](https://www.pseudepigrapha.com/pseudepigrapha/enoch.htm). "Mountain of Binding" fits 6:6, where Hermon is named from the oath.
- **Salt Vigil (understates, so the comment is wrong).** It says "the fire-and-salt image only". But the player text turns on looking back, which comes from Lot's wife: "looked back, and she became a pillar of salt" (Gen 19:26). Wisdom 10:7 itself names "a pillar of salt" as a monument "to an unbelieving soul" — [Bible Gateway, read](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+19:26;Wisdom+10:6-7;Exodus+8:3;Isaiah+14:9-11&version=NRSVUE). The Sodom moral is rightly absent.
- **Seated Kings (slightly firm).** Ugaritic rapiuma are invoked as dead kings to secure a successor; KTU 1.161 is the key liturgy. But interpretations range from deified ancestors to minor deities or human cultic figures — [Dead kings and Rephaim, snippet](https://relbib.de/Record/1650008201); [Rephaim text, snippet](https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/Rephaim%20text). The new-moon meal is not attested in what I reached.
- **Holt of Last Dew (accurate).** Vafthrudnismal 45 has the pair hidden in Hoddmimir's holt with morning dew as food — [Voluspa.org, snippet](https://www.voluspa.org/vafthrudnismal41-45.htm). Simek and Larrington take the holt as possibly Yggdrasil, not a literal wood — [Wikipedia, snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoddm%C3%ADmis_holt).
- **Unrecalled Chorus (accurate).** Frogs "into your palace, into your bedchamber and your bed" (Exod 8:3) — [Bible Gateway, read](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+19:26;Wisdom+10:6-7;Exodus+8:3;Isaiah+14:9-11&version=NRSVUE). Ovens were cut from the returned extract. "Old tales of Exodus" is an odd label for living scripture.
- **Paired Assize (evasive).** "World myth" hides a specific K'iche' source. The five paired offices (blood sickness, swelling, skeletons, stabbing from unswept corners, coughing blood on roads) and the houses, including a completely dark house, are in the Popol Vuh account — [Wikipedia, read](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xibalba). So "rooms and offices are all invented" is true of the wording, not the structure. "Room of No Lamp" is close to the Dark House.
- **Stiltwind Factors (acceptable).** The Met says Pazuzu "could be used to deflect" Lamashtu and links him to cold winds from the Zagros, while others say southwest — [Met Museum, snippet](https://metmuseum.org/toah/works-of-art/1993.181); [World History Encyclopedia, snippet](https://www.worldhistory.org/image/4900). So direction is disputed; the "Southwest Landing" city claims nothing, but a comment should not assert it. Comment's "feared as both cause and cure" is my reading of a secondary summary in the notes.
- **Ossuary Parliament (accurate, vague).** "the great owl and the raven shall live in it" (Isa 34:11) — [Bible Gateway, read](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+34:11-14;2+Kings+1:2;1+Samuel+5:4-5;Genesis+3:24&version=NRSVUE).
- **Peat Crown (accurate).** Shades roused to greet the ruler, "maggots are the bed beneath you" (Isa 14:9-11) — [Bible Gateway, read](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+19:26;Wisdom+10:6-7;Exodus+8:3;Isaiah+14:9-11&version=NRSVUE).
- **Refused Lanterns, Oilbearers.** Checked only against the notes' Wikipedia citations (secondary): Will the Smith and Stingy Jack; Forster's Mechanism. No contradiction.

**Gaps from the notes, now resolved**
- **Oisin poem.** *Laoi Oisin ar Thir na nOg* is usually dated about 1750; Coimin's authorship rests on indirect evidence and is unsettled — [OAC introduction, snippet](https://oac.ie/site/node/807); [NLI catalogue, snippet](https://catalogue.nli.ie/Record/vtls000065474). Write "about 1750, attributed to Micheal Coimin".
- **Baal-zebub.** Jewish Encyclopedia reads "Baal of flies": usually a god who drives flies off, possibly one who controls them; it finds the evidence for a corrupted "Zebul" insufficient — [Jewish Encyclopedia, snippet](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/2255-baal-zebub). Britannica notes Baal-zebul as an alternative reading — [Britannica, snippet](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Beelzebub). No people uses this name.
- **Dagon as fish god.** The fish root is treated as a folk etymology by most scholars, traced to Jerome and Kimchi; grain is favoured, not unanimously — [Encyclopedia.com, snippet](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/dagon); [New World Encyclopedia, snippet](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Dagon). 1 Sam 5:4-5 gives the fall and the threshold custom and says nothing of fish — [Bible Gateway, read](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+34:11-14;2+Kings+1:2;1+Samuel+5:4-5;Genesis+3:24&version=NRSVUE). This matters to Seven Mantles, which fuses a fish-cloaked teacher with the threshold story. That is good invention but must not be described as a source claim.
- **Manichaean dark kingdoms.** Five: Smoke, Fire, Wind, Water, Darkness in Theodore bar Konai's order; other lists vary — [Encyclopaedia Iranica, snippet](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/manicheism-parent/manicheism-pandaemonium/); Augustine runs from Darkness inward — [Against the Fundamental Epistle, snippet](https://bkv.unifr.ch/works/cpl-320/versions/against-the-epistle-of-manichaeus-called-fundamental/divisions/29). No people uses this.
- **Critias.** The MIT Jowett page has no Stephanus numbers, but confirms five pairs of twins, ten kings, the bull rite and orichalcum — [Jowett, read](http://classics.mit.edu/Plato/critias.html). A 121c reference for Zeus's speech appears in commentary — [blog, snippet](https://branemrys.blogspot.com/2014/06/critias.html). 113c remains unverified. No people draws on Atlantis directly, so no number is needed.
- **Amduat.** Twelve hours, solar barque, Apep in Hour 7 swallowing the waters, five doors in Hour 8, Maat's feather. Weighing of the heart comes after the journey in the Hall of Maat, not within an hour. Turquoise trees are not on this page — [Wikipedia, read](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amduat). So Night Barge's feather scale and blue-green trees should not be credited to the Amduat; the notes tie the trees to Duat material (secondary).
- **Abezethibou.** Two spirits uphold the pillar and swear they "will not let go this pillar until the world's end" — [Conybeare, read](http://www.esotericarchives.com/solomon/testamen.htm). Good fit for Tholekin.

## Does any player-facing text raise a respect concern?

### Takeaway
All player-facing strings in the 40 entries were read. No sacred figure is depicted and no sexual content or real disease name appears. The Lost Tribes, Solomon, Ahriman, Naga and jinn material in the notes was not used. One real concern is the Stiltwind Factors; three are lower-level (Manifest, Flensers, Ossuary Parliament). The Maya-derived Assize, and the Egyptian and Greek ones, are handled well.

### Cited Findings
- **Stiltwind Factors, moderate.** Not, I think, intended, but read together (local, `SealedPeoplesPlagueMarshes.cs` lines 47-49): a mercantile people suspected of calling the sickness for profit; "held half the world's debts"; "agents in every town"; towns that "drove out their agents"; and a sequel in which the wind drops once they are gone, confirming the suspicion. Long-beaked figures complete the picture. Compare the real history: Jews accused of spreading the Black Death by poisoning wells, with massacres following — [Jewish Encyclopedia, snippet](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/14858-well-poisoning.html); [Medievalists, snippet](https://www.medievalists.net/2022/07/medieval-well-poisoning-accusations-with-tzafrir-barzilay/); and modern claims of control over banks — [Wikipedia, snippet](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitic_trope). I did not reach ADL's own page.
- **The Manifest, low to medium.** "Cheerful ... islanders" under jungle, waiting for orders, guarding manuals they "cannot read", hoping for "cargo" from a ship. "Cargo cult" is widely criticised as a pejorative, a stereotype of confused people, applied to Melanesians, and movements such as John Frum on Tanna are living communities — [Wikipedia, snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_cult); [Lindstrom/OpenEdition, snippet](https://books.openedition.org/pacific/170). The crew's descent from real spacefarers softens this, and it is my inference that players would make the link.
- **Flensers of Ummoth, low to medium.** "heavy-jawed" and "The diet has changed their faces" judge a people by physiognomy, in a whaling and hide-tent culture that could be read onto Arctic or Pacific whaling peoples (my judgement). "the old tales promised her flesh" echoes the rabbinic banquet of Leviathan, hide tents included — [Jewish Encyclopedia, via notes, secondary](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/9841-leviathan). No scripture is quoted; the echo is acceptable, the faces line is not needed.
- **Ossuary Parliament, low.** Birds who "perform the funerals of any people", who nest in towers, with a "Tower Eleven", echo living rites: Parsi towers of silence, and Tibetan sky burial still practised at a few sites — [Atlas Obscura, snippet](https://atlasobscura.com/articles/sky-burials-towers-of-silence-birds-corpses-asia); [Harvard Divinity Bulletin, snippet](https://bulletin.hds.harvard.edu/under-a-birdless-sky). The tone is respectful and the birds are not named after any faith, so the fix is small.
- **Paired Assize, acceptable.** Christenson reportedly links the lords' duties to present-day K'iche' belief that illness comes from underworld lords, so the source is living belief, not just old myth — [UVA Multepal, snippet](https://multepal.spanitalport.virginia.edu/node/3336). Player text has no Maya name, place, ball game, jaguar or bat; the justices are invented and the court is coded to a marsh. Keep it that way, and do not add Mesoamerican dress in portraits.
- **Night Barge Guild, Choir of the Hush: acceptable.** Egyptian and Greek images, invented names, no gods shown (local). Apep is a surge, not a character. Nothing is sacred-as-villain.
